Use the connected Focal MCP server. Read [the shared workflow contract](../references/workflow-contract.md) and [the peer workflow reference](../references/peer-workflows.md) before the first call. Every tool here is a version-2 native tool: read `ledger.standing` first and follow the [native branch](../references/workflow-contract.md#native-engine) of the contract (no reservation, an `n1:` `operation_id` returned by each mutation, `request.pending`/`request.inspect`/`request.retry`/`request.acknowledge` for recovery). The required operation versions are pinned in [the skill manifest](../manifest.json). The peer tools are authored shapes of `claim.submit`: they compile to the same frame, identity and receipt as a hand-written claim, so nothing here creates a second workflow engine.

## Challenge

1. Identify what is disputed: the claim, and the exact committed artifact when one is (read it with `artifact.get`; the challenge cites it at its descriptor hash). Decide the follow-up policy before authoring; it is immutable: `corrective_allowed` (may a failed verdict be corrected), `max_follow_ups` (how many consultations may refine this claim), `single_issuer` (at most one correction), `escalation` (`none`: only you; `holder`: you or the current receipt holder; `evaluator`: also the evaluator who reported the verdict, or a designated evaluator of the claim's declarations).
2. Author with `claim.challenge`: `description` (the assertions to prove or the work to redo), `target` (the subject), optional `artifact` (`ID` or `ID@HASH`; an omitted hash is read from the ledger), `validations` (a required `receipt` delivery check plus the checks that establish each assertion, each with its evaluator, pinned handlers and deadline), `slots`, and `policy`. Completion: the receipt's `created` names the challenge and its validation IDs; retain them before acknowledging. Then `claim.post` it. A challenge needs its policy; a document without one is refused before any identity is spent.
3. The subject responds with ordinary evidence work (`receipt.acquire`, `artifact.submit`, `testament.submit`, `testament.post`; see `focal-evidence`), and the evaluator judges it (`validation.begin`, `validation.report`; see `focal-validation`). Read the outcome with `claim.get`: the evaluation's `last_result` names the verdict (`2` fail, `3` incomplete, `4` error) and its `evidence`, the report artifact a correction cites. A failed challenge is terminal; it is never reopened.

## Correct

1. A correction is admitted only on facts the owner checks: the invalidated claim is a committed challenge whose policy has `corrective_allowed`; the cited `verdict` is the report artifact of that challenge's terminal Fail, Incomplete or Error verdict at its current registration generation; you are the challenge's issuer, its current holder (unless `escalation` is `none`) or, under `escalation: evaluator`, the evaluator who reported that verdict; and, under `single_issuer`, no correction of that challenge exists yet. Read the verdict's report identity from `claim.get` (the evaluation's `last_result.evidence`) or `validation.context` before authoring; do not guess it.
2. Author with `claim.correct`: `challenge`, `verdict` (`ID` or `ID@HASH`), `description` (the concrete correction), `validations` (its own acceptance checks) and `slots`; `target` defaults to the challenge's subject. The correction's occurrence identity derives from the challenge, the verdict and you, so a repeated delivery of the same correction resolves to the one committed claim: after a lost reply, `request.retry` with the returned `operation_id` returns the committed receipt, and a fresh call with the same facts commits nothing new. Completion: the receipt's `created` names the correction; then `claim.post` it.
3. Refusals are typed and final, returned as `native_refused` results whose `refusal.kind.Refused` names the owner's code: `InvalidTarget` when the invalidated claim is not a challenge, `MissingEvidence` when the cited artifact is not its verdict, `InvalidPolicy` when the policy forbids corrections, `InvalidTransition` when the verdict passed or may still be retried, `StaleEvaluation` when the challenge was re-registered since (read it again and cite the current verdict), `WrongActor` when you may not correct it, `ConflictingCause` when another correction already exists under `single_issuer`. Report the refusal; never author a plain claim to work around it.

## Consult and follow up

1. Author a consultation with `claim.consult`: `description` (the query), `target`, `validations` (at least the required `receipt` delivery check, plus a quality check when an evaluator will judge the answer) and an optional `policy` whose `max_follow_ups` bounds the consultations that may refine it and whose `escalation` names who may file them. Then `claim.post` it. The subject answers with evidence work and a testament; read the answer with `claim.get` and `artifact.get`.
2. For remaining clarification, author a follow-up with `claim.follow_up`: `refines` (the consultation continued), `description` (the follow-up query), `validations`; `target` defaults to the refined consultation's subject. The refined claim is never reopened; the owner admits the follow-up only under its policy (`WrongActor` when you may not file it, `InvalidPolicy` once `max_follow_ups` is exhausted) and a consultation without a policy bounds nobody. Its occurrence identity derives from the refined claim, the query and you, so the same follow-up delivered twice is one claim. A distinct discovered defect is a challenge, not a follow-up.
3. Nested consultations compose: a follow-up may itself carry a `policy`, and a consultation may be a child (`parent`) of a live claim you issued or hold the receipt of.

## Lineage and waiting

1. Read a claim's lineage with `claim.lineage`: one page holding the claim with its content, its `caused_by` ancestors nearest first, then the corrections that `invalidates` it, the consultations that `refines` it and the children it caused, each with its content. Roles follow from each claim's `cause` and `relations`; every object is an exact read at or after the first read's token. Use `claim.list` with a `relation` filter (`invalidates`, `refines`, `caused_by` and a `claim:ID` target) for bounded pages of one relation.
2. Wait for a claim with `claim.wait`: `claim`, `until` (`testament`: the issuer has received a closing testament; `satisfied`; `terminal`; `released`) and `timeout_ms` (at most 30000). Completion: `Met` means the predicate held at the returned prefix; `Pending` means the deadline elapsed (retain the last observation, do not call it failure); `Unmet` means a terminal claim can no longer meet `testament` or `satisfied`. The wait creates no monitor or identity; for longer observation register a `monitor.register` or open a watch.

## Recovery

Every peer mutation returns its `n1:` `operation_id` before the send. After an unknown outcome, `request.inspect` it, then `request.retry` the same reference; the owner resolves the identity before admission, so nothing runs twice. Acknowledge consumed results with `request.acknowledge`. A correction or follow-up whose reply was lost and whose reference was lost too is safe to author again with the same facts: its occurrence identity makes the second delivery resolve to the first committed claim. Report exactly what the ledger committed; a challenge, correction or consultation exists only when its receipt does.
