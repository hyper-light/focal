# Focal comprehensive codebase audit — 2026-09-29

**Verdict:** Focal has a substantial protocol, persistence implementation, and adversarial test suite. It also has reproducible availability and recovery defects, a native client that runs out of capacity after ordinary cumulative use, and several architectural ceilings that prevent the current implementation from meeting its global-scale objective. Passing formatting, Clippy, dependency checks, and most integration tests does not close these issues. Fix the concrete failure paths first; qualify capacity and failure recovery before making stronger operating guarantees.

This audit covers the product as a whole: domain semantics, native and legacy engines, evidence, storage, Raft, ranges, routing, admission, clients, CLI, MCP, participant runtime, enrollment, deployment, observability, release tooling, and verification. Raft and global scale receive additional depth, rather than displacing the other areas.

**65 dated finding records: 35 initial findings, 19 in the first extension, and 11 in the continuation (4 P1, 7 P2).** These are dated evidence, not a claim that every finding remains open after concurrent fixes. Sections 14–18 deepen QUIC, Raft, allocation/reallocation, page faults, recovery, and poor-network analysis. Section 19 adds demonstrated follower-read failure, Raft ingress starvation, recovery-work and funding mismatches, participant connection churn, and consumer lifecycle/copy costs; it also refreshes concurrent fixes. Same-configuration recovery, native retry-journal lifecycle/races, byte-funded ingress, and post-revocation dispatch remain immediate priorities, alongside shared-connection cancellation and the newly reproduced availability defects. Continuous operation additionally needs founder renewal and historical-state reclamation; global operation needs bounded delegated cells and component-level availability guarantees.

## 2. What the product actually does

Focal is an **agent coordination protocol and evidence ledger**. A claimant directs work to a respondent and declares what acceptance requires. A receipt establishes responsibility or delivery; it does not establish correctness. The respondent performs work outside Focal, attaches immutable evidence, and supplies a testament, including diagnostic evidence when work fails. The designated evaluator records results against the exact evidence and declaration. Satisfaction is derived from those committed facts. Native claims, artifacts, validations, evaluations, receipts, testaments, scopes, and monitors have distinct lifecycles; terminal state and release are separate facts.

The service does not execute arbitrary agent tools. `focal-runtime` is an optional participant-side embedding for handlers, with retry and reconciliation contracts. Treating its absence from a daemon execution path as a missing scheduler would misunderstand the product.

One binary supplies the service, CLI, and stdio MCP adapter. Local operation uses credentialed local IPC and a private data directory. Remote operation uses enrolled identities, authenticated QUIC, committed topology and placement, and independent session logs. Raft establishes ordering and durability; native records materialize deterministic committed state. Reads establish a quorum boundary or use explicit prefix/lease semantics. Content custody is separate from WAL durability: storing a reference in a replicated log does not make the referenced evidence durable on the promised fault domains.

The intended global architecture is **many independently ordered session ledgers**, partitioned discovery and placement, and geographically bounded operating units. Range materializers can spread a large ledger's state and verification work; they do not eliminate its single sequencing order. One laptop should preserve the same protocol semantics with a smaller physical topology and a weaker explicitly reported durability guarantee.

Relevant contracts include [domain and lifecycle](../archictecutre/02-domain-and-lifecycle.md), [storage and distribution](../archictecutre/04-storage-and-distribution.md), [native lifecycle storage](../archictecutre/18-lifecycle-storage-upgrade.md), [native input](../archictecutre/21-native-input-format.md), [native records](../archictecutre/22-native-record-format.md), [activation and import](../archictecutre/23-native-activation-and-import.md), [placement and fleet control](../archictecutre/24-placement-execution-and-fleet-control.md), [parallel materialization and ranges](../archictecutre/25-parallel-materialization-and-ranges.md), and [custody, archive, and restore](../archictecutre/26-custody-archive-retention-and-restore.md).

Several substantial capabilities already exist and must not be proposed as if absent:

- Native activation/import, decoder floors, and deterministic replay are implemented. V1 compatibility is intentionally frozen.
- Range split, merge, movement, holder integration, chunked checkpoint seeds, archive retirement, and restore are connected to ledger/node paths. The range README is stale.
- Raft already has pre-vote, quorum checking, learners, joint configurations, leadership transfer, conflict handling, bounded inflight replication, ReadIndex, snapshots, priorities, and measured path/timing machinery.
- Shared physical WAL ownership and batching across logical groups exist. The fleet shares bounded workers rather than creating an unrestricted thread and WAL per session.
- Native follower materialization uses up to four workers in the production network composition; the leaf default of one is not the production setting.
- Managed V1 request generations already support bounded retirement/rotation. That does not imply equivalent native journal lifecycle support.
- There are real binary, QUIC, multi-node, crash-cut, custody, movement, history, and deployment tests. The problem is incomplete coverage of particular failure/resource boundaries and scale qualification.

## 4. Correctness, recovery, and resource admission

### F01 — P1: peer-loss feedback can turn retryable refusal into owner termination

**Evidence:** [fleet feedback and progress](../../crates/focal-node/src/fleet.rs), `report_lost`/`progress_group`; [grouped fleet error handling](../../crates/focal-node/src/fleet_group.rs), lines 421–429; [control owner](../../crates/focal-node/src/control_host.rs), lost-feedback/tick loop; [consensus guard](../../crates/focal-consensus/src/lib.rs), `report_unreachable`/`guarded_in`; [consensus reservation errors](../../crates/focal-consensus/src/memory.rs), lines 13–22; [replication driver](../../crates/focal-node/src/replication.rs), lines 136–146.

At the initial audit checkpoint, the driver posted a lost-peer notification after `PeerSendError::Lost`. `progress_group()` drained those notifications **before** checking/draining outstanding persistence. `report_unreachable()` went through the consensus mutation guard, which correctly returned `PersistencePending` while a Ready/checkpoint/decoder write was outstanding. The owner propagated that result with `?`; the grouped worker logged the error and stopped the session. The control owner similarly propagated lost-peer reports outside the tick's retryable-error handling and exited its run loop.

An isolated probe held the real shared WAL owner behind a replay callback, proposed an entry, and obtained `None` from `try_drain()`. Reporting peer 2 returned `Err(PersistencePending)` with `failed=false`. Releasing the writer then committed the entry normally. The consensus core was healthy; the then-current host converted a temporary refusal into termination.

The full suite built before the concurrent retry fix failed `placement_agent::tests::a_dead_voter_is_drained_and_replaced_without_waiting_it_out`. Its log reported the founder's session stopped with “durability is outstanding” and then failed to heal. A targeted rerun failed again with the same stop and unavailable founder replica. These are observed availability failures, separate from the initial sandbox socket errors. The evidence and fleet QUIC failover tests also failed in that full run but passed isolated reruns; those additional failures are timing-sensitive evidence, not three independently established root causes. Other enclosing tests passed despite logging the same stop, exposing a verification gap.

**Concurrent remediation, refreshed during the extension:** session/control loops gained retry handling and a paused-WAL session regression test. The current session branch also retains **`LedgerError::Consensus(ConsensusError::Capacity)`**, as well as pending durability, host capacity, and memory refusal. The nested consensus-capacity omission present at the first report checkpoint has been corrected. Section 12 preserves the earlier executions; section 14 distinguishes current source review from those historical results.

The earlier queue/reinsertion path lacked actual deduplication and could silently lose a retained report. Current `report_lost()` instead drains into a bounded sorted peer set, retains peers through retryable refusal, and counts coalescing/overflow. That source remedy closes the earlier reinsertion mechanism. Feedback remains a bounded hint; deterministic pressure and control/session coverage are still the appropriate closure evidence.

**Fix:** retain a bounded deduplicated set of pending peer feedback; apply it only when the group can mutate; retain entries on retryable capacity/persistence refusal; poll durability first and process a bounded number per worker slice. Count coalescing/overflow explicitly. Classify temporary host errors consistently for session and control owners.

**Acceptance:** deterministically pause the WAL, deliver dial/time-out loss feedback during Ready and LightReady, resume it, and verify both owners remain live and commit. Repeat during checkpoint/decoder-floor writes, membership changes, memory pressure, and duplicate feedback. Dead-voter replacement must pass without unexpected owner-stop diagnostics; a wider timeout does not fix the cause.

### F02 — P1: retirement can create a checkpoint that the same configuration cannot restore

**Evidence:** [retirement publication](../../crates/focal-core/src/native/retirement.rs), lines 679–807, particularly 751–754; [checkpoint count validation](../../crates/focal-core/src/native/record_codec/read_validate.rs), lines 195–254; [committed retirement application](../../crates/focal-ledger/src/native_session_apply.rs), lines 397–404.

`retire_native_family()` increments `meta.outcomes` and writes an outcome and new prefix, but does not enforce `self.limits.outcomes`. Recovery requires the outcome count to fit that limit and equal the native prefix. This creates an admission/recovery asymmetry.

The isolated native Core probe used `outcomes=3`, then created, cancelled, and released a claim. Retirement returned `Ok(14)` and advanced to prefix 4 with four outcomes. A structurally valid checkpoint could be encoded. Restoring it with the unchanged limits returned **`Contract(Capacity)`**. The small limit exercises the same code and comparison as the default million-outcome limit; a million-operation production run was not performed.

**Fix:** account for the retirement outcome before proposing/publishing retirement, including outstanding completion promises. Define deterministic behavior for a committed system record whose operation no longer fits. Rejecting only after durable commitment, stopping the owner, or publishing a state recovery rejects are inadequate. Existing over-limit states need an explicit supported repair/migration decision.

**Acceptance:** boundary tests at limit−1, limit, and limit+1; multiple families; pending completion reservations; WAL-only replay and checkpoint recovery; no publication on refusal. A permitted retirement must restore with exactly the same resource configuration and preserve every original request receipt.

### F03 — P1: network allocations precede the budget intended to bound them

**Evidence:** [frame buffer allocation](../../crates/focal-wire/src/frame.rs), lines 61–73 and 202–224; [QUIC request handling](../../crates/focal-wire/src/transport.rs), lines 488–516; [connection accounting](../../crates/focal-node/src/network_listener.rs), lines 294–299; [production limits](../../crates/focal-node/src/control_host.rs), lines 481–489; [node budget](../../crates/focal-node/src/network_service.rs), line 502.

An authenticated stream validates a frame header, then allocates and zero-fills the **entire announced payload** before any payload arrives and before `dispatch_accounted()` performs request admission. Nested decode allocations also occur before dispatch. The listener's 64 KiB connection charge is not a permit for these per-stream payload buffers.

Concurrent byte-buffer codec work now checks encoded lengths against available input and uses fallible exact byte allocation; its compatibility/malformed-length tests pass. That is useful decode hardening. It does not acquire the missing process/tenant ingress permit or change the full announced frame allocation above.

A header-only probe announcing 10 MiB allocated **10,485,760 bytes** while its payload reader remained pending. The production control limit permits 10 MiB frames. `for_consensus(128)` supplies a much larger stream allowance than the default 16; the relevant configured path permits roughly 1.4 GiB of application payload buffers on one fully occupied connection, before decoded objects and other owners. The production node budget is 1 GiB; generated pods have a 2 GiB memory limit. The aggregate allocation is a capacity calculation, not an executed OOM attack.

QUIC's stream receive window is already capped at 1 MiB: it should not be misreported as a 10 MiB window per stream. That transport improvement does not cap the application allocation above. Progress-aware payload waits also let a sender retain a full buffer by delivering 1,200 bytes per wait. At 30 seconds per wait, a 10 MiB frame can occupy memory for about 73 hours. Supporting slow useful transfers is reasonable; unpriced occupancy is the defect.

**Fix:** acquire a funded process/tenant/principal ingress-byte permit after the bounded header and before allocation; conservatively fund decoded shape; independently reserve control progress; bound stream admission by bytes as well as count. Release all permits on cancellation and malformed input. Price partial-transfer occupancy and useful progress without replacing every transfer with an arbitrarily short fixed deadline.

**Acceptance:** thousands of in-limit headers with absent/slow payloads must stay within a measured RSS envelope and leave node control/completion traffic live. Test nested vectors, decode refusal, revoked identities, stream cancellation, and role-incompatible requests. Compare actual allocations/RSS to accounted bytes, not just the budget counter.

### F04 — P1: normal native use exhausts the client journal after 256 lifetime operations

**Evidence:** [native journal limits and accounting](../../crates/focal-client/src/native_store.rs), lines 79–89, 455–461, and 599–627; [CLI store composition](../../crates/focal-node/src/cli/native.rs), lines 300–332; [native driver](../../crates/focal-native-client/src/driver.rs), lines 109–177.

The default native journal allows 256 operations. Capacity is computed from catalogue entries, not unresolved work. `record_delivered()` marks a journal delivered but never removes or archives the catalogue entry or returns its reservation. Ordinary CLI composition consistently selects the default limits; the journal interface is also used by the native MCP path.

A one-slot probe prepared an operation, recorded its committed receipt, and marked it delivered. `outstanding()` returned zero; usage remained one; the next unrelated prepare returned `Capacity`. Thus the default 256 is a **cumulative-use cutoff**, rather than a bound on concurrent unknown outcomes. A long-running participant reaches it while operating correctly.

**Fix:** provide a native journal lifecycle with durable acknowledgment, independently fenced generations, retention of exact-result lookup, and safe archival/reclamation. Managed V1 has useful lifecycle precedent, but native identity/outcome semantics need their own reviewed contract. Deleting the directory or raising 256 merely postpones or undermines retry guarantees.

**Acceptance:** ordinary CLI and MCP workflows must run well beyond the default window across process restarts and concurrent processes while keeping bounded live storage. Lost committed replies must still return the identical receipt; an old operation identity must never become an unrelated new operation.

### F05 — P2: expansion failure leaks a slot that pending-operation discovery cannot show

**Evidence:** [native prepare ordering](../../crates/focal-client/src/native_store.rs), lines 467–487 and 664–678; [resolution/compilation callback](../../crates/focal-native-client/src/driver.rs), lines 130–177.

Before resolving/compiling input, the store durably inserts an entry with `ready=false`. If the expansion callback fails, the claim remains. `outstanding()` skips not-ready entries. With an automatically generated operation ID, the driver returns the expansion error rather than the claimed identity. A missing object, temporary read failure, or compilation error can therefore consume permanent capacity without exposing a recoverable pending operation to the caller.

An isolated failed-expansion probe returned an error, zero outstanding operations, and catalogue usage one. No request had been sent.

**Fix:** make unexpanded identities discoverable with their authored intent and explicit recoverable state, or reclaim a proven never-ready/never-sent identity through a safe journal transition. Preserve crash recovery between identity claim and prepared-frame publication. Surface an assigned identity when work needs recovery.

**Acceptance:** repeatedly fail resolution before frame publication; occupancy must remain bounded/recoverable and the caller must be able to inspect every durable claim. Inject crashes at claim/prepared/ready boundaries; assert no frame escapes before durable Ready.

### F06 — P1: native journal updates have a cross-process time-of-check/time-of-use race

**Evidence:** [record_reply, record_delivered, and record_refusal](../../crates/focal-client/src/native_store.rs), lines 561–661; [directory lock ownership](../../crates/focal-client/src/operation_store/files.rs), lines 44–48 and native directory opening.

Each method calls `retry()`, which opens/locks the store, reads state, and returns after that lock is dropped. It then separately opens/locks the directory to write a journal derived from the stale state. Serialization of individual reads and writes does not serialize the transition.

Concrete interleaving: A reads “no receipt” in `record_refusal`; B records a committed receipt; A reacquires the lock and overwrites the journal with `receipt=None`, `delivered=true`, and a refusal. This directly contradicts the method's promise that a committed receipt is never overwritten. A delayed `record_reply` can likewise overwrite a newer delivered journal with generation 1 and `delivered=false`. Exact server-side retry identity may preserve the business outcome, but it does not excuse losing or regressing durable client knowledge.

**Fix:** hold one directory lock through reading the current journal, validating the transition, and durably replacing it. Enforce monotonic generation and committed/delivered precedence against the state read under that lock. Do not solve this with a process-local mutex alone.

**Acceptance:** deterministic multi-process interleavings for reply/reply, reply/delivered, reply/refusal, and crash during replacement. Once a matching committed receipt is durable it must remain the receipt forever; delivered state must never regress. No race reproduction was executed in this audit; the lock gap and overwrite path were inspected directly.

### F35 — P1: slow request bodies retain authority captured before certificate revocation

**Evidence:** [QUIC stream authentication and dispatch](../../crates/focal-wire/src/transport.rs), lines 508–516; [authenticated dispatch](../../crates/focal-wire/src/handler.rs), lines 121–155; [grant snapshots](../../crates/focal-wire/src/auth.rs), `PeerRegistry::authenticate`; [ordinary service routing](../../crates/focal-node/src/managed_service.rs), lines 300–313; [current revocation test](../../crates/focal-wire/src/tests.rs), lines 383–439.

Each stream clones an `AuthenticatedPeer` from the registry **before waiting for the header/body**. After the body arrives, `dispatch_accounted()` verifies the request against that old grant without consulting the registry again. Revocation removes the registry entry; it does not invalidate an already cloned peer. The ordinary managed service repeats the tenant check against the same snapshot. Specialized founder enrollment control has additional current-authorization checks; this finding does not assert those are bypassed.

A real loopback QUIC probe negotiated the normal protocol, sent only a request header, waited 100 ms, then revoked the actor certificate before sending any request payload. The registry denied a fresh authentication and the handler call count was still zero. Completing the payload then invoked the handler once and returned its response: `registry_denies=true, handler_calls=1`. This demonstrates post-revocation dispatch, not committed mutation or disclosure of real tenant data. A new stream started after revocation is refused, as the existing test verifies; the uncovered interleaving is revocation while a stream is still receiving.

F03's progress-based body wait makes this more than a tiny scheduling race: a peer can pre-open incomplete requests and retain the captured grant through a long useful-progress window. Changed tenant/role grants and expiry projection updates have the same stale-snapshot issue.

**Fix:** reauthenticate against the current local grant projection immediately before request dispatch/admission, after receipt of the complete frame; define and enforce any authorization lease/revision through subsequent queued admission. Make grant invalidation release waiting resources. Specify the treatment of work already durably accepted separately from incomplete network requests, preserving exact-result recovery. Distributed revocation has a propagation delay; an installed local revocation should not be ignored because a peer sent an earlier header.

**Acceptance:** revoke or restrict a grant during header receipt, partial body, queued admission, and exact retry; verify no newly admitted unauthorized request or tenant data is released. Test all ordinary participant roles and the specialized control/custody paths. Previously committed identities must retain safe recovery semantics under the explicit authorization policy.

## 5. Native queries, participant interfaces, and historical proof

### F07 — P2: evaluation continuation drops the boundary evaluation

**Evidence:** [evaluation page](../../crates/focal-node/src/native_reads.rs), lines 500–545.

The page skips `wire_key <= after`. Once the result count reaches `max_items`, it sets `next` to the current `wire_key` **before returning that evaluation**. A caller that supplies this continuation therefore skips that evaluation on the next page.

For ordered evaluations A, B, C and `max_items=1`: page one returns A and continuation B; page two starts strictly after B and returns C. B never appears. This follows directly from the two comparisons; it was not independently exercised through a populated network fixture.

**Fix:** define the cursor in terms of the last fully consumed key, or explicitly use an inclusive continuation. Prove iteration order matches cursor comparison, including declaration changes, target variants, missing rows, and generations.

**Acceptance:** concatenating pages at sizes 1, 2, and larger must equal an unpaged result without skips/duplicates, at one pinned prefix. Include exact-boundary pages, deleted/retired rows, and all evaluation targets.

### F08 — P2: the compiler selects a “current” evaluation from only the first page

**Evidence:** [resolution](../../crates/focal-native-client/src/driver.rs), lines 77–106; validation-context selection around 294–349; [evaluation selection](../../crates/focal-native-client/src/resolve.rs), lines 393–442; [server limits](../../crates/focal-core/src/native.rs), lines 219–223.

The driver requests evaluations with `after=None` and `READ_ITEMS=256`, extends objects from that page, and ignores `next`. The resolver then chooses the highest non-terminal matching generation **among those objects**. The core allows up to 4,096 evaluations per claim, so a valid current evaluation can be beyond this page. The caller can get a false missing-current error or bind an older still-live matching evaluation. Validation-context construction has the same first-page selection pattern.

This becomes particularly likely with many increments, targets, or superseded generations. Increasing `READ_ITEMS` does not provide bounded correctness for the larger supported domain.

**Fix:** prefer a server query for the exact target/current evaluation, backed by an index. Otherwise follow bounded continuation pages at a pinned prefix and report explicit capacity/incompleteness if the work bound is reached. Never interpret an incomplete page as the complete selection universe.

**Acceptance:** place the selected evaluation after item 256; exercise whole-work slots, admission, increments, several non-terminal generations, and concurrent publication. CLI and MCP must compile against the intended binding or return a truthful bounded-read refusal.

### F09 — P2: schema examples are normalized by the legacy decoder and rejected by native input

**Evidence:** [discovery commands and example normalization](../../crates/focal-node/src/cli/discovery.rs), lines 22–54 and 497–524; [strict native DTOs](../../crates/focal-client/src/operations/native_documents.rs); [native CLI document loading](../../crates/focal-node/src/cli/native_documents.rs).

`get schema` has a native selector, but `get example` does not and normalizes through `operations::parse_json`, the V1 decoder. The generated `claim.submit` example contains `evidence_schemas`; native parsing rejects it as an unknown field. The isolated probe reproduced this with the current raw example and its actual typed normalization. Removing one incompatible field does not establish that the remaining deadline and native declaration semantics are correct.

Shape-only validation and operation name discovery similarly use the legacy operation registry, making native-only coverage and examples inconsistent. The current discovery tests validate examples against the registry that produced them, so they can pass while the native command rejects the result.

**Fix:** select the engine/version consistently for catalogue, schema, example, and local validation. Generate examples from the same contracts used by the selected CLI/MCP mutation path. Provide examples for native-only verbs and explicit compatibility output where needed.

**Acceptance:** pipe every generated native example through its real document decoder, compiler, and appropriate fixture-backed CLI/MCP path. At minimum, the advertised fresh-native quickstart must work verbatim without hand-editing generated JSON.

### F10 — P2: lineage output hides truncation and conflates a lower-bound observation with one prefix

**Evidence:** [lineage observation](../../crates/focal-native-client/src/observe.rs), lines 16–19, 61–172.

Lineage traversal is deliberately bounded to 16 ancestors and 64 related claims. It makes fresh relation-list requests with no cursor continuation, ignores list continuations, and drops extra related claims. The returned `NativeReadPage` has `next=None`, with no indication of either omitted ancestors or followers.

The first claim is linearizable; subsequent object reads use `AtLeast(first_token)`, and relation lists may observe still newer state. The final page carries the **first** token, native sequence, and logical time alongside those later objects. Its comment describes the lower-bound approach, so this is not evidence that the server's Exact or linearizable reads are broken. It is an interface ambiguity: a normal page-shaped value can be mistaken for a complete observation at one prefix.

**Fix:** expose a distinct composite observation type with explicit minimum/observed prefixes and truncation/continuations, or make the traversal genuinely pinned to one exact snapshot. Keep the work limits; make their effects visible.

**Acceptance:** deep chains, more than 64 followers, empty filtered pages with continuations, and writes during traversal. Output must distinguish complete lineage from a bounded sample, and every displayed prefix claim must match its stated semantics.

### F11 — P2: archival proof survives, but ordinary participant object resolution does not

**Evidence:** [object reads](../../crates/focal-node/src/native_reads.rs), lines 203–246; [retired document](../../crates/focal-node/src/native_documents.rs), `retired`; [generic content download](../../crates/focal-node/src/content_host.rs), lines 833–853; [retirement](../../crates/focal-core/src/native/retirement.rs).

Claim reads can return `NativeObject::Retired` with the archive bundle continuation. Artifact, definition, evaluation, and result reads look only in live Core rows; after family retirement they produce missing objects. The native client does not hydrate those rows from the bundle or transparently resolve the exact historical bindings through a dedicated archive query.

The bytes are not proven lost: generic tenant-scoped content download can fetch a known archive reference, and operator archive inspection exists. The gap is the participant-facing way to follow a previously accepted artifact/validation ID and recover the typed proof it refers to. A missing live object should not imply that accepted historical proof never existed.

**Fix:** offer an authorized, versioned archive continuation for historical object identities, plus bounded typed hydration or proof queries. Preserve identity, lineage, original prefix, schema verification, and custody checks. Make online versus archival latency explicit.

**Acceptance:** complete a two-party workflow, retain every exact binding, retire the family, restart/move the ledger, then retrieve its artifact, evaluation, result, and testament through normal client/CLI/MCP interfaces. Test denied tenant access and unavailable archive custody separately from unknown objects.

### F33 — P2: the native participant surface leaves existing content-transfer machinery inaccessible

**Evidence:** [native payload documents](../../crates/focal-client/src/operations/native_documents.rs), lines 938–944; [artifact compilation](../../crates/focal-native-client/src/compile.rs), lines 518–580; [native CLI routing](../../crates/focal-node/src/cli/native.rs), lines 235–241 and 907–909; [MCP catalogue composition](../../crates/focal-mcp/src/stdio.rs), lines 136–143; [file payload loading](../../crates/focal-node/src/cli/native_documents.rs), lines 33–57.

Their existence does not make them usable through the normal native CLI/MCP workflow. This also forces evidence bytes into authored JSON/frame/journal paths that could otherwise hold a small immutable descriptor. **Acceptance:** native CLI and MCP upload, attach, and download valid file evidence; resume interrupted transfers and lost seals; preserve exact mutation identity after restart; refuse wrong schema/domain/class and missing custody. Measure journal/frame memory and replicated bytes with inline versus referenced evidence, including payloads near every interface/schema boundary.

### F34 — P2: native atomic creation batches stop at the core boundary

**Evidence:** [native command vectors](../../crates/focal-core/src/native.rs), lines 493–502; [authored batch checks](../../crates/focal-core/src/native/authored_prepare.rs), around lines 231–252; [single-claim compiler](../../crates/focal-native-client/src/compile.rs), lines 1511–1529; [authored operation inventory](../../crates/focal-client/src/operations/native_documents.rs); [CLI batch refusal](../../crates/focal-node/src/cli/native.rs), line 80.

## 6. Lifetime capacity, credentials, and storage performance

### F12 — P1: outcome retention makes native ledger capacity cumulative

**Evidence:** [default native limits](../../crates/focal-core/src/native.rs), lines 214–215; [request outcome admission](../../crates/focal-core/src/native/prepare.rs), lines 657–659; [retirement exclusions](../../crates/focal-core/src/native/retirement.rs), lines 117–137; [checkpoint count/prefix contract](../../crates/focal-core/src/native/record_codec/read_validate.rs), lines 249–254.

Every fresh native request increments the retained outcome count. The default limit is 1,000,000. Retirement removes live family rows but intentionally retains outcomes and creation results for exact retries; it also creates another outcome. Checkpoint validation requires outcome count to equal the native prefix. Consequently, freeing evidence/lifecycle rows does not restore fresh mutation capacity.

At a hypothetical 1,000 fresh mutations/second, one million outcomes is consumed in 16 minutes 40 seconds; at 100/second, about 2 hours 47 minutes. These are arithmetic illustrations, not measured throughput. Existing completion reservations must be preserved; this finding does not assume they are absent. The problem is an indefinitely used session's lifetime history becoming its active capacity bound, compounded by F02.

**Fix:** design exact historical receipt lookup and negative retry fences independently from resident live mutation state. Possible mechanisms include reviewed epoch/generation sealing with archived receipts, partitioned outcome storage, or a bounded resident index over durable historical proof. This changes a recovery invariant and requires a versioned contract; deleting dedup rows or simply enlarging the limit is unsafe/inadequate.

**Acceptance:** many more lifetime mutations than the live outcome window, with retirement, restart, leadership change, and historical exact retries. Live memory and admission should track unresolved/live obligations; historical proof must remain recoverable without re-executing old work.

### F13 — P1: the founder has an expiry horizon without a renewal/rotation lifecycle

**Evidence:** [default credential lifetime and founding issuance](../../crates/focal-enrollment/src/registry.rs), lines 23–31 and 547–588; [bootstrap PKI](../../crates/focal-enrollment/src/pki.rs), lines 56–80 and 117–125; [founder credential recovery](../../crates/focal-node/src/network_bootstrap.rs), lines 309–319; [renewal controller](../../crates/focal-node/src/network_controller.rs), lines 1172–1181 and 1346–1354; [credential renewal interface](../../crates/focal-node/src/credential_renewal.rs), lines 1–6 and 53.

The founder's **node credential defaults to 30 days**, using `EnrollmentLimits::credential_lifetime`. An isolated founding-enrollment probe produced that lifetime and returned `Expired` at its expiry boundary. The bootstrap enrollment server certificate is separately issued for 365 days and persisted in `authority.bin`; the CA is issued for ten years. The founder's automatic maintenance is explicitly skipped, and explicit founder renewal returns `Unsupported`. Startup also uses the persisted founding receipt and requires it still to authorize. The controller's retirement check applies to the founder as well. Reopening the authority does not replace its certificate. The probe verifies issuance/authorization behavior; a 30-day running cluster was not exercised.

Joined credential renewal/key rotation is implemented and should receive credit. It does not solve the founder's expiry, bootstrap enrollment endpoint's expiry, or CA rollover. A product meant to run continuously needs a supported path before these clocks expire, including after an outage or clock advance.

**Fix:** make founder credentials ordinary renewable node credentials where appropriate, separate bootstrap enrollment server identity from immutable genesis proof, and support issuer/root-trust succession and issuer recovery. Keep public committed authorization distinct from private signing-key custody. Do not defeat expiry checks or extend certificate lifetimes as the operating plan.

**Acceptance:** advance controlled time beyond the initial credential lifetime; renew ahead of expiry, restart before/after renewal publication, enroll a new participant afterward, and verify old certificates retire at the committed grace boundary. Exercise CA handoff, lost issuer recovery, and founder replacement with the original cluster identity intact.

### F14 — P1: shared-WAL checkpointing creates node-wide I/O and disk-headroom coupling

**Evidence:** [physical checkpoint owner](../../crates/focal-log/src/writer.rs), lines 1131–1172; [rewrite implementation](../../crates/focal-log/src/lib.rs), lines 356–395; [default checkpoint cadence](../../crates/focal-node/src/fleet.rs), `ReplicaConfig::new`.

A logical-group checkpoint reserves transient disk space equal to the **whole current physical WAL**, streams every unrelated group's record into a new generation, writes the target checkpoint, installs the fence, scans the new generation again, and cleans up the old generation. The shared physical writer performs this work synchronously, so it blocks other groups' append/replay commands until it finishes. Async receipts free fleet workers; they do not make this disk owner concurrent.

With many active groups reaching their 4,096-entry checkpoint cadence, each can repeatedly rewrite all other groups. This amplifies bytes read/written and couples a small session's compaction to the largest neighboring histories. A near-full disk can cleanly refuse checkpointing even when compacting that one group would release space, because the replacement requires whole-log headroom. The clean refusal is good; the operating envelope is poor.

**Fix:** retain shared batching but introduce segmented retention with per-group durable floors and a durable manifest. Reclaim obsolete segments incrementally, or coordinate/checkpoint multiple groups per generation. Run bounded background compaction with I/O quotas and preserve crash-safe old/new generations until publication. Avoid reverting to one unrestricted physical writer per logical session.

**Acceptance:** many hot/cold groups, a large old WAL, constrained free space, concurrent heartbeats/appends, and crashes at every manifest/fence/cleanup cut. Measure checkpoint bytes per reclaimed byte, append p99, and maximum extra disk required. A small group's checkpoint should not force unrelated history-sized work indefinitely.

### F15 — P2: Raft catch-up allocates for the suffix rather than the requested page

**Evidence:** [stable entry fetch](../../crates/focal-consensus/src/storage.rs), lines 383–407; [unstable log slice](../../crates/focal-raft/src/log.rs), lines 521–550.

Stable fetch reserves `high-low` entry slots before applying `max_bytes`. Unstable slicing copies the entire selected suffix, then calls `limit_bytes()`. The returned page is small, but its temporary allocation and retained vector capacity are not bounded by that page.

The public log probe used 1,024 unstable entries with 4 KiB payloads and a 4 KiB page budget. It returned one entry but requested **4,268,032 allocated bytes** and retained capacity for **1,024 entries**. This is total allocator-requested bytes during the operation, not a measurement of process RSS or a release throughput result.

Repeated catch-up pages can repeat suffix-sized copying, approaching quadratic work over a lagging replica's backlog; many peers and an inflight window amplify the pressure. The performance guard then has to budget that unnecessary peak.

**Fix:** select the byte-limited prefix before allocating/cloning; retain Raft's at-least-one-entry behavior for an oversized first entry. Reserve only the selected count, and combine stable/unstable byte accounting correctly. Preserve owned buffers required by the Ready/persistence contract.

**Acceptance:** allocation counts/bytes and retained capacity for long stable, unstable, and mixed tails; page budget zero/small; first oversized entry; snapshots and compaction. Catch-up temporary memory should scale with the page and bounded metadata, not the whole backlog.

### F16 — P2: heartbeat-sized work requests history-sized staging and accounting

**Evidence:** [staging calculation](../../crates/focal-consensus/src/memory.rs), lines 128–149; [guarded mutations](../../crates/focal-consensus/src/lib.rs), lines 1389–1405; [persistence staging](../../crates/focal-consensus/src/persistence.rs), lines 74–116; [resident accounting](../../crates/focal-consensus/src/storage.rs), `resident_bytes`.

Every guarded tick, campaign, read barrier, report, or incoming step computes a conservative allowance containing twice resident history, per-member batch/snapshot space, and multiples of raw state. Resident-byte helpers also inspect retained collections. Even a small control transition can require substantial temporary headroom and history-counting CPU. As logs grow, the node may refuse control progress at capacity although the logical operation is tiny.

This guard exists to cover real copies and should not be weakened blindly. F15 removes one reason for its large allowance, but operation-specific funding remains necessary for a predictable control lane. The claimed liveness consequence here is a static capacity risk; no threshold/heartbeat stall benchmark was performed.

**Fix:** maintain checked resident-byte counters incrementally; precompute exact bounded outgoing work; distinguish tick/read/report staging from proposal/snapshot staging; fund a minimum control transition separately. Bound the number of history walks per tick and verify transient peaks empirically before reducing reservations.

**Acceptance:** fixed-size heartbeat/read/report operations over increasing histories and under near-limit ordinary pressure. Control p99 and required free bytes should have an explicit bound; failure must be retryable and must not terminate owners.

### F17 — P2: the persistence pipeline leaves avoidable latency on the Raft critical path

**Evidence:** [Ready/LightReady state machine](../../crates/focal-consensus/src/persistence.rs), lines 225–380; [WAL append fence](../../crates/focal-log/src/lib.rs), lines 517–524 and 610–625; [mutation fence](../../crates/focal-consensus/src/lib.rs), lines 1383–1387.

The current implementation intentionally prevents further group mutation while its durable Ready is outstanding. Ready records persist before advance; a LightReady commit change can require another ordered hard-state write before committed application output is released. A single-voter mutation can therefore require two physical durability cycles. A physical cycle syncs active data, the replacement fence file, and directory publication, subject to platform-specific implementation. Shared group commit amortizes cycles across groups; it does not remove the two-phase dependency within one group.

Leader outbound replication is also released through this durable event path. Established Raft can overlap leader disk persistence with follower replication while ensuring the leader's own match index represents durable entries, and follower acknowledgments remain persistence-dependent. That is an implementation redesign, not permission to send durable success early. [Ongaro's dissertation, section 10.2](https://web.stanford.edu/~ouster/cgi-bin/papers/OngaroPhD.pdf) describes the relevant overlap and batching principles.

**Fix:** first measure Ready-to-fence, fence-to-send, quorum-to-apply, and client-journal latency separately. Evaluate an asynchronous Ready contract with safe immediate versus persistence-dependent messages, bounded group commit by both bytes and latency, and a reviewed single-voter commit/fence optimization. Keep read publication and exact receipts behind the required durability boundary.

**Acceptance:** crash cuts before/after each disk fence and message class, disk stalls, concurrent election/configuration changes, and black-box exact-result histories. Benchmark release binaries on representative filesystems. Do not delete `fsync`, let speculative self-acks count toward quorum, or relax publication to obtain a favorable microbenchmark.

### F18 — P2: immutable event history participates in hot lifecycle page copying

**Evidence:** [namespace selection](../../crates/focal-core/src/native.rs), lines 1075–1100; [row-copy logic](../../crates/focal-core/src/native/prepare.rs), lines 121–124 and 148 onward; [existing allocation qualification](../qualification/allocations-2026-09-29.md).

Definitions, artifact descriptors, claim content, creation results, legacy rows, and indexes have separate page namespaces. `Meta`, mutable claim/lifecycle rows, events, and outcomes still fall into namespace 0. A COW write touching hot state can therefore copy immutable event rows sharing an affected page; events and claims have fallible owned-copy paths, including dynamic content.

The existing allocation report is useful corroboration of allocation costs, but its dated measurements are not fresh measurements from this audit. This is a remaining layout opportunity after prior descriptor/page improvements, not a claim that all native state is deep-cloned for every operation.

**Fix:** separate immutable event/outcome history from hot lifecycle/control pages; tune page layouts and affinity using measured touched-page counts. Preserve checkpoint layout/proof compatibility or version the affected layout. Prefer shared immutable pages and borrowed views over adding a reference-counted owner to every tiny row.

**Acceptance:** lifecycle updates over large retained histories must copy only a bounded hot working set; compare allocated bytes, page clones, resident peaks, and replay/checkpoint hashes for serial and parallel materialization.

### F19 — P2: evidence downloads repeat whole-manifest work for every byte page

**Dated status:** the behavior below describes the original checkpoint. Concurrent cached-plan/search remediation addresses that mechanism; section 19.1 records current source, a passing focused regression, and remaining accounting/concurrency limits.

**Evidence:** [range reads](../../crates/focal-evidence/src/store.rs), lines 768–825; [manifest verification](../../crates/focal-evidence/src/store.rs), lines 1010–1044; [download dispatch](../../crates/focal-node/src/content_host.rs), lines 833–853.

Each `read_range()` reloads, hashes, decodes, and validates the whole manifest, then scans chunk offsets from the beginning until it reaches the requested range. Downloading an N-chunk object as small successive pages performs O(N²) cumulative manifest/chunk-metadata work, besides repeated manifest I/O. It verifies the needed data chunks correctly; the cost of locating them is the issue.

Node custody transfers already have useful retained-manifest machinery, and `read_verified` now reuses a scratch buffer. Those improvements should not be re-reported as missing. The ordinary paged-download path still repeats the work above.

**Fix:** bounded authenticated manifest caching or a leased download plan, with cumulative offsets and direct/binary-searched chunk lookup. Separate response page size from stored chunk indexing. Cache validation provenance under the immutable manifest digest, and continue verifying each delivered data chunk.

**Acceptance:** sequential and random ranges on increasing chunk counts; allocation/I/O totals proportional to transferred bytes plus bounded/indexed metadata work; corrupt/replaced chunk files, lease expiry, and cold cache. Tenant authorization must precede any reusable content plan.

## 7. Cluster admission, authority, deployment, and operations

### F20 — P1: the global connection cap prevents the admission policy from doing its job

**Evidence:** [node listener outer cap](../../crates/focal-node/src/network_listener.rs), line 294; [generic QUIC listener](../../crates/focal-wire/src/transport.rs), line 344; [identity admission](../../crates/focal-wire/src/admission.rs), lines 1–47; [default wire limit](../../crates/focal-wire/src/message.rs), line 785.

Admission separates pending handshakes from authenticated identities and replaces an identity's least-used connection at its own limit. The outer listener counts **all** live connection futures and refuses newcomers at `max_connections` before authentication or identity replacement can occur. Thus full established occupancy also consumes the opportunity to authenticate a replacement.

With default limits, eight participant identities holding 16 connections each can fill the 128 outer places. A restarting node or a participant replacing its own stale connection cannot reach identity-aware admission. This does not require one identity to bypass its 16-connection bound. Enrollment shares the node listener's outer bottleneck as well.

**Fix:** bound pending handshakes separately from established connections, with explicit aggregate memory accounting and node/control headroom. Let the bounded handshake stage identify an eligible same-identity replacement even at active occupancy. Reserve classes fairly, including enrollment, without making the pending pool an unbounded back door.

**Acceptance:** fill participant capacity, restart a voting peer, replace a same-principal connection, enroll a node, and run half-open/malicious handshakes concurrently. Existing healthy peers and required reconnection must retain bounded latency; all refusals must be observable.

### F21 — P1: root authority and cluster topology remain global whole-state bottlenecks

**Evidence:** [authority state and defaults](../../crates/focal-directory/src/authority.rs), lines 48–80; [authority snapshot](../../crates/focal-control/src/authority.rs), lines 210–225; [grant/route installation](../../crates/focal-node/src/network_controller.rs), lines 305–331 and 1475 onward; [liveness membership](../../crates/focal-node/src/liveness/driver.rs), lines 36–45.

Directory partitions and delegation exist, but infrastructure authority still maintains node and log-group maps with defaults of 1,024 nodes and 4,096 groups and an 8 MiB checkpoint. Partition authority snapshots clone the full infrastructure registry and enrollment checkpoint. Controllers build certificate grants and contact routes from the full corresponding state; liveness tracks a 1,024-member map.

These are sensible bounds for an operating unit; they are not a global control plane. The current root-level assignment/refresh path is the central constraint even if session records are independent and directory row storage is partitioned. Liveness uses SWIM-style bounded probes/gossip; this finding does not allege naive all-to-all broadcast. It concerns global membership materialization and authority distribution.

**Fix:** make the existing bounded unit an explicit cell. Delegate infrastructure group/node authority and enrollment to region/cell authorities with proof of parent authorization and versioned succession. Route through regional directories and bounded caches, distribute incremental revisions, and keep local liveness neighborhoods. The global root should manage bounded cell authority/delegation records rather than every session and machine.

**Acceptance:** add cells without growing every node's resident node/group/certificate map; operate a cell through parent unavailability within explicit lease/authority rules; fence revoked cell epochs; recover and converge routes after moves. Raising these constants is not proof of global scalability.

### F22 — P1: identity churn eventually exhausts enrollment even at low live occupancy

**Evidence:** [enrollment bounds](../../crates/focal-enrollment/src/registry.rs), lines 13–45; invitation checks at 763/824; certificate check at 846; persisted records and changes at 96–146 and 442 onward.

The default registry bounds invitations and enrollments at 4,096, with an absolute invitation ceiling of 65,536. Invitation records remain after consumption, revocation, and expiry, and enrolled identity records remain in the authoritative registry. Expired previous certificates from renewal have their own grace cleanup; that does not reclaim old invitations or historical identities.

At global scale, ordinary machine replacement, failed onboarding, short-lived actors, and revocations are steady-state behavior. A bound on cumulative issued records turns a security/administrative history into permanent fresh-admission failure even when the active population fits comfortably.

**Fix:** partition issuance and authorization; compact closed invitation generations under durable rejection fences; separate active authorization from immutable issuance/revocation history. Preserve exact redeem retries and prevent old tokens/certificates from regaining meaning. Tie cleanup to committed state and issuer epochs, not wall-clock deletion alone.

**Acceptance:** lifetime onboarding far beyond one active-window bound, mostly expired/revoked/consumed invitations, repeated restart, token replay, grace-overlapping rotations, and concurrent joins. Active authorization and checkpoint sizes must remain bounded while historical audit evidence remains available.

### F23 — P2: every authority change pays for unrelated topology state

**Evidence:** [authority prepare](../../crates/focal-directory/src/authority.rs), lines 225–295, especially 277–282; [control snapshot estimate](../../crates/focal-control/src/authority.rs), lines 216–236.

Preparation reserves the full current authority state plus extra bytes, then deep-clones `self.root.state`, including all node and group maps. Even `AdvanceClock` takes this path. A stream of node renewal/topology/group updates therefore costs O(total nodes + groups) per change. Copying and serializing whole authority snapshots adds another growing cost downstream.

**Fix:** persistent/paged map roots or bounded owned deltas with atomic publication; indexed expiration/clock work; incremental snapshot distribution. Preserve revision lineage, compare fences, and rejection rollback. The COW work already done in other stores does not automatically cover this registry.

**Acceptance:** one changed grant and clock advancement over increasingly large registries; measure allocation, copied bytes, checkpoint bytes, and preparation latency. Cost must reflect changed/touched pages, with unchanged topology shared safely.

### F24 — P1: data-plane zone survival does not establish control-plane zone survival

**Evidence:** [deployment changes](../../crates/focal-node/src/deployment/plan.rs), lines 69–84; [KIND campaign's metadata result](../qualification/campaigns/2026-09-29-kind.md), section “Defect D5”; [cluster administration](../cluster-admin.md).

The deployment planner changes policy and plans session placement. It does not include a corresponding root-voter durability plan. The recorded KIND campaign obtained three session voters across zones while root metadata remained a single founder voter and the other hosts remained learners. Losing the founder preserved session read availability but interrupted metadata/placement/membership operations until it returned.

Manual root promotion exists. It is not part of a verified whole-product guarantee returned by session placement. Nor does root promotion alone provide private issuer availability: public metadata replication and certificate signing-key custody have different failure requirements.

**Fix:** model and report separate guarantees for session ordering, range state, evidence/archive custody, directory partitions, root infrastructure authority, and issuer service. Expand/enforce root and required partition voter placement when a deployment requests fault-domain survival. Make any remaining weaker issuer/recovery requirement explicit in the achieved guarantee and blockers.

**Acceptance:** after the deployment reports the requested zone/region guarantee, lose each required domain in turn and exercise existing reads/writes, new session creation, placement repair, credential renewal, enrollment, and archive lookup. Availability must match the actual component-level promise.

### F25 — P2: readiness is wired to the deliberate process-alive probe

**Dated status:** the original identity-only readiness wiring has been replaced by an owner-serving predicate. Section 19.1 records its actual scope; broader availability guarantees are not implied by that predicate.

**Evidence:** [Kubernetes renderer](../../crates/focal-node/src/deployment/render/kubernetes.rs), lines 415–420; [Helm probes](../../deploy/helm/focal/templates/_helpers.tpl), lines 151–165; [alive probe semantics](../../crates/focal-node/src/cluster_admin.rs), lines 1060–1073.

Startup, liveness, and readiness all execute `cluster node probe --check alive`. That probe intentionally reads only identity and avoids root/session/placement state. This correctly prevents a missing quorum from provoking a liveness restart cascade. It also means a pod with stopped serving owners can remain Ready while its process/admin identity is responsive—particularly relevant to F01.

**Fix:** retain the independent alive liveness probe. Add serving readiness based on the node's intended role and required local owners, initialization, fencing, and routing/custody state. Do not require every follower to be leader or make transient quorum loss cause liveness failure. If peer discovery must include not-ready pods, make that service setting deliberate and separate from client readiness.

**Acceptance:** alive but stopped session/control owner; learner catch-up; initial enrollment; fenced binary; healthy follower; temporary quorum loss. Readiness must reflect the defined serving contract without causing supervisor-induced quorum collapse.

### F26 — P2: large session populations outgrow the fixed metrics view

**Evidence:** [session metrics ceiling](../../crates/focal-node/src/metrics.rs), line 19; [sampling](../../crates/focal-node/src/network_service.rs), lines 1231–1238; `sessions_truncated` rendering in [metrics](../../crates/focal-node/src/metrics.rs).

Add bounded histograms for queue delay, fsync/fence, quorum, apply, custody, ingress occupancy, retry reasons, and client-observed latency. **Acceptance:** put the only failed/hot group outside the first 512; aggregate metrics and alerts must still reveal it. Memory/cardinality bounds must hold at thousands of groups and under identity/session churn. Accounted network bytes should be compared with real RSS.

### F27 — P3: the production no-panic gate permits local opt-outs

### F28 — P1: qualification currently supports correctness journeys, not the requested global capacity claim

**Evidence:** [capacity envelope](../qualification/capacity-envelope.md); [dated allocation report](../qualification/allocations-2026-09-29.md); [workload driver](../../tools/load/src/driver.rs); [workload shape](../../tools/load/src/shape.rs); [nightly gates](../../.github/workflows/nightly.yml); [CI](../../.github/workflows/ci.yml).

## 8. Participant runtime, memory mechanics, and hot-ledger architecture

### F30 — P2: stuck participant handlers cannot be forcibly cancelled or resource-contained in process

**Evidence:** [handler worker pool](../../crates/focal-runtime/src/pool.rs), lines 44–86 and 130 onward; runtime `Executor`, cancellation, and reconciliation contracts.

Workers execute synchronous callbacks on OS threads. Cancellation is cooperative. A handler that never returns can occupy a worker indefinitely; dropping the pool detaches an unfinished thread instead of blocking shutdown. Its allocations, external calls, and CPU are outside the ledger's bookkeeping permits. A late external effect can continue after the logical attempt deadline, so the reconciliation contract is essential.

This is an intentional embedding constraint and does not mean the Focal daemon executes untrusted agents. It matters when integrations use this pool for arbitrary or failure-prone tools and assume timeout restores worker capacity or stops side effects.

**Fix:** offer a supervised process/container executor option with bounded concurrency, memory/CPU, kill/reap behavior, and durable run/attempt identity. Keep the in-process path for trusted cooperative code and document its resource guarantees precisely. External effects still require idempotence/reconciliation; killing a process does not prove an effect never occurred.

**Acceptance:** hung, cancellation-ignoring, allocating, and crashing handlers; all-worker exhaustion; restart while an external outcome is unknown. Other work must retain its promised capacity, and unknown effects must never be automatically repeated without the required proof.

### F31 — P3: arena page-directory growth has quadratic cumulative copying

**Dated status:** concurrent geometric-directory growth addresses the algorithmic defect below. Its focused capacity/accounting regression passed; section 19.1 distinguishes capacity changes from measured allocator calls.

**Evidence:** [arena growth](../../crates/focal-memory/src/arena.rs), lines 309–323.

Adding one page allocates a new directory of length `old_len+1`, moves every existing page descriptor into it, and replaces the old directory. Over P pages this moves O(P²) descriptors and requests a new directory on every page. Payload pages themselves are not copied, so this is a smaller metadata cost, but it becomes visible for very large arenas.

**Fix:** grow the directory with a bounded, charged capacity policy and `try_reserve` before mutation, or use a segmented directory. Preserve failure atomicity, peak-memory accounting, stable generational handles, and the non-reuse guarantee.

**Acceptance:** increasing arena sizes must show near-linear cumulative directory work and a bounded transient peak; allocation refusal must leave the old directory and handle behavior intact. Existing generation-exhaustion tests remain essential.

### F32 — P1: range parallelism does not remove a hot session's sequencing ceiling

**Evidence:** [range/materialization contract](../archictecutre/25-parallel-materialization-and-ranges.md), sections 1, 2, and 7; [native range bound](../../crates/focal-core/src/native.rs), `max_ranges: 64`; [production worker setting](../../crates/focal-node/src/network_service.rs), lines 1865–1880; [fleet worker](../../crates/focal-node/src/fleet_group.rs).

Admission and ordering remain serial at the session leader. Native follower staging can run in dependency waves, and range holders can hold subsets, but the session's ordered log still reaches its members. A holder is a learner of that session, not a new independent write sequencer. The default 64-range bound and up-to-four replay workers provide finite parallelism, not unbounded throughput for one globally hot session.

This is compatible with the product's independently ordered-session architecture. The missing operating contract is quantitative: maximum sustainable mutations, evaluation/receipt fanout, retained rows/history, read rate, and recovery time **per session and per cell**, including a single hot affinity/control namespace. Adding nodes cannot be assumed to multiply a hot session's write capacity.

**Fix:** make independently ordered sessions/cells the primary scale unit. Define supported hot-ledger limits and overload behavior, and measure when splitting state helps versus when sequencing, control affinity, WAL, or log delivery dominates. Where workloads need a single global order, state its latency/throughput cost explicitly. Introducing multi-leader or cross-session atomicity changes protocol semantics and needs a separate design, not an opportunistic performance patch.

**Acceptance:** hot single claim, many independent claims within one session, many independent sessions, dense dependency graphs, and large evidence verification, with local and WAN quorums. Publish both the single-session ceiling and fleet aggregate results; all advertised configurations must preserve exact ordering and acceptance semantics.

For globally distributed linearizable writes, place each ordinary session's quorum according to its explicit fault-domain guarantee and workload locality. A geographically distributed majority incurs network latency determined by the quorum that must actually participate; removing this cost while preserving the same semantics is not a transport tuning knob. The [Raft paper](https://raft.github.io/raft.pdf) describes majority-based commitment and configuration safety. Use asynchronous cross-cell discovery/read projections only where their staleness is explicit; keep acceptance and exact-result authority with the ordered session.

## 10. Scale qualification and operating design

### 10.1 Required scale units and invariants

The workable global design is a hierarchy of bounded units:

1. **Laptop:** one node/disk; honest disk-loss durability; bounded native journal generations; bounded evidence; recoverable checkpoints; useful admission under memory/disk pressure.
2. **Cell:** a bounded set of nodes and independently ordered ledgers; local infrastructure/enrollment authority; a fault-domain-aware metadata quorum; shared WAL/owner isolation with measured capacity; bounded routing and liveness neighborhoods.
3. **Region:** discovery and placement over cells, explicit delegated authority and proof succession, locality-aware ownership, aggregate quotas, and bounded caches. A regional failure cannot require scanning every global session on every surviving node.
4. **Global:** a bounded directory of regional/cell authority, placement policy/residency constraints, historical proof discovery, and operations that explicitly define which failures/latencies they tolerate. Global session/node identity should not imply global all-node resident state.

Delegation, split/move, and partitioning already provide pieces of this design. The work is completing authority/enrollment locality, measuring shared physical bottlenecks, and proving operation at the unit boundaries. “Meta scale” should be expressed as sessions/second, active and historical sessions, evidence bytes/second, identities/day, concurrent reads, regions/cells, p99 latency, repair RTO, and retained proof horizon. The repository currently cannot honestly supply all those achieved figures.

Preserve these contracts through optimization:

- One accepted mutation has one exact durable outcome; uncertain transport completion never becomes a different request identity.
- Committed application publication is a complete prefix, including parallel materializers and moved holders.
- Exact evidence/validation identities survive archival and remain retrievable through authorized interfaces.
- Completion obligations remain funded while ordinary admission is refused.
- Stale route, placement, incarnation, decoder, and authority epochs cannot regain authority after cleanup.
- Metadata ordering, content custody, and private signing-key custody have independently checked guarantees.
- Disk/memory/connection limits govern actual retained work, including before decoding and during compaction/recovery.
- Losing one cell or region does not force an unbounded global repair burst or invalidate unrelated cells' valid authority.

### F36 — P1: useful progress cannot overcome the outer exchange deadline

**Evidence:** [peer pool](../../crates/focal-wire/src/peers.rs), defaults and `send_bounded`, lines 784–909; [Raft limits](../../crates/focal-consensus/src/lib.rs), lines 111–113, 584, and 943; [payload arrival](../../crates/focal-wire/src/frame.rs), lines 192–225.

Every Raft operation is non-bulk. The default pool applies a **five-second deadline to the complete exchange**, including peer-lane waiting, connection acquisition, request transfer, and response. Inner progress-aware waits cannot extend that deadline. The accepted entry ceiling is 4 MiB; snapshots permit 8 MiB. Payload alone requires approximately **6.71 Mbit/s** for 4 MiB or **13.42 Mbit/s** for 8 MiB within five seconds, before framing, RTT, contention, and retransmission. These are arithmetic lower bounds, not measured throughput. Small appends can work on slower links; a sufficiently large valid atomic entry cannot. Native seeded checkpoints already avoid putting their complete Core root into one snapshot, but that does not eliminate the transport's general atomic-message limit.

Incoming bodies also require 1,200 bytes, or the final remainder, within each arrival wait. A non-final body therefore has a useful-progress floor of approximately **1,920 bit/s** with custody's five-second period and **320 bit/s** with the ordinary 30-second period. A scaled public-API probe supplied 40 bytes every 20 ms with a 200 ms wait: the reader timed out after 201 ms despite 360 useful bytes arriving continuously. This demonstrates the quantum mechanism, not a measured production link.

**Fix:** give bounded queue/dial waits, transfer stalls, and peer processing their own contracts. Fund retained bytes before allocating; detect the affected stream's useful progress; make large snapshots resumable. Separate normal append page size from the accepted atomic-entry ceiling. A large atomic command needs a separately funded bounded transfer/reassembly path; splitting it into unrelated committed commands changes semantics. Preserve finite resource occupancy and typed refusal when the offered workload cannot fit the path.

**Acceptance:** actual framed pool traffic at 100 bit/s and 1/8/64/256 kbit/s, with asymmetric capacity, jitter, loss, competing streams, and outages. Exercise tiny, mixed, and maximum permitted messages. Useful completion must be possible within the declared operating contract, genuine stalls must release permits, and slow useful progress must not revive F03's unfunded retention.

### F37 — P1: one unavailable operation closes every stream on the peer connection

**Evidence:** [failure handling](../../crates/focal-wire/src/peers.rs), lines 873–895; [physical remote close](../../crates/focal-wire/src/transport.rs), lines 745–746.

`Response::Error(Unavailable | OutcomeUnknown)` falls through to `remote.close()` and cache invalidation. Several request-local wire failures take the same path. The physical connection carries unrelated sessions, replication, probes, and custody. One owner that is temporarily unavailable can therefore cancel healthy work, force handshakes, restart congestion learning, and amplify repeated bytes across groups.

A real loopback QUIC probe warmed one cached connection, dispatched and held a healthy sibling request, then returned `Unavailable` from another handler. The refusal and already-dispatched sibling both returned **`Err(Lost)`**, with one connection opened. The outer pool timeout itself cancels its exchange before this close branch; this probe specifically establishes the completed operation-error path.

**Fix:** retire/reset the affected stream and preserve the healthy connection for operation refusal or an unknown result. Close the connection on connection failure, current authentication/route retirement, or a reviewed connection-wide health decision. Retry the same request identity with bounded jittered scheduling; distinguish resource refusal, slow processing, route change, stream loss, and peer loss in feedback.

**Acceptance:** one group's `Unavailable`, `OutcomeUnknown`, or timeout must not abort another dispatched reply, replication acknowledgment, probe, or partially transferred chunk. Measure connection openings, repeated bytes, controller warm-up, commit tails, and unknown-outcome recovery during repeated isolated failures.

### F38 — P2: connection-wide UDP activity is not this stream's delivery

**Evidence:** [carried accounting](../../crates/focal-wire/src/transport.rs), lines 146–189, particularly 167–169; response/request uses at 520–521 and 814–816.

`carried()` credits `udp_tx.bytes - path.lost_bytes` from the whole connection to each exchange. Other streams, transport overhead, and retransmissions do not establish acknowledged unique bytes for this request. Newly discovered losses can also decrease the derived value, after which positive movement is credited again from the reduced baseline. A busy higher-priority stream can give a stalled lower-priority exchange false delivery credit and send it prematurely into its short response wait; unrelated traffic can also prolong a stalled exchange's retention. This is an inspected accounting mechanism, not an end-to-end congestion measurement.

**Fix:** distinguish queued, accepted by transport, acknowledged, received, and answered progress for the specific exchange. If Quinn's stream APIs do not expose the required acknowledgment boundary, use bounded application progress or reviewed instrumentation. Do not infer delivery from aggregate UDP counters.

**Acceptance:** hold one stream flow-control blocked while another transmits continuously; add loss discovery, retransmission, reordered acknowledgments, and priority pressure. The blocked stream must acquire neither false delivery credit nor indefinite occupancy.

### F39 — P2: explicit ECN feedback can leave Copa's controller unchanged

**Evidence:** [Copa adapter](../../crates/focal-wire/src/congestion.rs), lines 421–433 and 503–511; [Quinn 0.11.18 controller contract](https://docs.rs/quinn-proto/0.11.18/quinn_proto/congestion/trait.Controller.html).

Quinn represents an ECN-triggered congestion callback with `lost_bytes == 0`. Focal discards `_lost_bytes` and invokes the ordinary loss handler. In default noncompetitive mode, a nonpersistent event changes neither the congestion window nor slow start. A probe issued five explicit ECN callbacks: the window stayed **12,000 bytes**, slow start stayed true, and inverse delta stayed 2. That demonstrates ignored callback semantics; it does not measure an ECN-enabled network or prove Copa generally performs poorly.

**Fix:** specify an explicit CE response compatible with Copa's delay/random-loss design. Test it against short-queue AQM and competing controllers. Do not indiscriminately treat all random loss as congestion or assume Copa must copy NewReno; [RFC 9002](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9002.html) permits alternative controllers, and the [Copa paper](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/conference/nsdi18/nsdi18-arun.pdf) describes its delay-based design.

**Acceptance:** CE-marked, minimally dropped traffic during slow start and steady state; measure queue delay, useful goodput, marks, loss, and fairness with NewReno/CUBIC. Persistently signaled congestion must produce the declared response.

### F40 — P2: stream slots are reserved, but useful bandwidth is not

**Evidence:** [traffic classification](../../crates/focal-wire/src/message.rs), lines 249–258 and 318–331; [stream priorities](../../crates/focal-wire/src/transport.rs), lines 514 and 774–797; [pool lanes](../../crates/focal-wire/src/peers.rs), lines 323–339.

All opaque Raft messages, including large appends and snapshots, receive control priority 10 alongside votes, heartbeats, probes, and control RPCs. Equal-priority streams take turns, so a large recovery population can delay tiny critical messages. Higher-priority traffic consumes available bytes before lower-priority content; sustained control demand at link capacity gives custody no useful bandwidth guarantee. Separate semaphore lanes reserve stream occupancy, not link capacity. Required evidence custody and decoder/seed recovery are prerequisites for useful service, even though they use bulk streams.

**Fix:** classify verified outbound Raft messages by type/size into bounded critical exchanges, ordinary replication, and recovery transfer. Use fair target/group byte admission with reserved critical capacity and bounded progress for required recovery/custody. Keep offered-load admission explicit when the link cannot meet all promises. Existing priority tests are useful; replacing strict priority universally without measurement is premature. [RFC 9000 §2.3](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9000.html#section-2.3) leaves application prioritization to the implementation.

**Acceptance:** recovering and healthy groups sharing a connection at 8/64/256 kbit/s. Measure tiny message tails and positive unique custody progress under sustained control pressure, reverse-capacity limits, and checkpoint storms. Include cross-tenant fairness and hard byte/memory bounds.

### F41 — P2: Raft's inflight count is not an inflight byte budget

**Evidence:** [inflight ring](../../crates/focal-raft/src/progress.rs), lines 26–55 and 218; [append construction](../../crates/focal-raft/src/raft.rs), lines 405 and 464; [consensus defaults](../../crates/focal-consensus/src/lib.rs), lines 111–113 and 584; [pool limits](../../crates/focal-wire/src/peers.rs), line 66.

A tiny append and a 4 MiB append each occupy one of 128 inflight slots. The normal append-page ceiling is set from the accepted atomic-entry maximum plus 1 KiB. The nominal count window can represent roughly 512 MiB per follower at maximum size; memory admission can refuse far earlier, so this is **not observed retained memory**. Neither uncommitted proposal bytes nor transport packet congestion control independently bounds committed backlog queued for a slow learner. A fixed count cannot provide an efficient working set across laptop loopback, a 64 kbit/s path, and a large bandwidth-delay product.

**Fix:** separate accepted entry size, target append page size, inflight message count, and inflight payload bytes. Track byte credits with acknowledged end indexes and release them on acknowledgment/state transition. Limit selection before allocation/copy; derive an application byte target from path estimates and budget. Handle one oversized atomic entry without deadlock or altered semantics, and reserve critical responses outside the data window. [etcd Raft's configuration](https://github.com/etcd-io/raft/blob/main/raft.go) and [inflight implementation](https://github.com/etcd-io/raft/blob/main/tracker/inflights.go) provide a primary reference for independent message/byte bounds.

**Acceptance:** mixed sizes, slow minority/learner, snapshot transitions, changing capacity, joint membership, and stale/duplicate/out-of-order acknowledgments. Assert conserved credits, bounded queued bytes, no spin on an oversized first entry, and healthy-majority commit progress.

### F42 — P1: a slow cached peer can fill the global driver with waiting futures

**Evidence:** [replication driver](../../crates/focal-node/src/replication.rs), lines 117–157; [production driver capacity](../../crates/focal-node/src/network_service.rs), lines 1464–1471; [peer permit ordering](../../crates/focal-wire/src/peers.rs), lines 784–822 and 905–908; [group queue](../../crates/focal-node/src/fleet_group.rs), line 13; [frame offering](../../crates/focal-node/src/fleet.rs), lines 3086–3152.

The driver counts a send future as globally active before that future awaits a per-peer permit. With 1,024 driver futures targeting one peer and 128 per-peer permits, 896 waiting futures can still fill the driver. It then stops receiving healthy targets queued behind them. Failed-dial cooldown does not solve this: the slow peer may have an open cached connection. Separate ledger/control drivers reduce coupling between those drivers, but each can suffer the same starvation. This is a concrete scheduling mechanism; a full 1,024-frame campaign was not executed. It is P1 for the requested many-group availability envelope because one non-quorum recipient can obstruct healthy groups' majority traffic.

**Fix:** obtain target credits before promoting work into the active global send set. Keep bounded per-target queues under global byte/item funding, fairly schedule targets and groups, and preserve a critical-message allowance. Coalesce only messages with proved term/group/recipient supersession. Preserve snapshot failure identity and report admission failure to the owner; do not create optimistic progress by silently dropping frames.

**Acceptance:** fill queues for a cached peer that never answers, then enqueue healthy-majority heartbeats, votes, and appends. They must progress independently before the slow-peer timeout. Repeat across hundreds of groups, route retirement, cancellation, full credits, and shutdown; assert payload and item accounting conservation.

### F43 — P2: ReadIndex confirms the same burst repeatedly

**Evidence:** [read broadcasts and confirmation](../../crates/focal-raft/src/raft.rs), lines 1484–1509 and 1537–1547; [prefix release](../../crates/focal-raft/src/read.rs), lines 111–134; [host read correlations](../../crates/focal-node/src/fleet.rs), lines 2831–2841 and 2900–2933.

The core already releases older reads when the latest context is confirmed. It nevertheless immediately broadcasts for each request. A public RawNode probe elected a three-voter leader, committed the current-term no-op, and queued 20 unique reads: **40 heartbeat frames** were produced. One follower's acknowledgment of the latest context released **all 20 barriers**. This establishes redundant confirmation traffic and the existing coalescing capability, not a measured application throughput gain.

**Fix:** batch reads already received within a bounded owner slice and issue one latest-context round, with individually correlated required applied indexes. A quiet read should leave immediately; bound batch size without introducing an arbitrary batching timer. A read arriving after the confirmation was sent must receive a new proof. Keep term, configuration, authorization, route, readiness, and applied-prefix fences. Give pending reads an independent bound. [Ongaro's dissertation §6.4](https://web.stanford.edu/~ouster/cgi-bin/papers/OngaroPhD.pdf) describes amortizing leadership confirmation; clock leases require additional assumptions.

**Acceptance:** 32/128 concurrent reads, single-read latency, writes racing reads, leader loss between enqueue/acknowledgment, joint configuration, and eligible follower reads. Prove histories remain linearizable while broadcasts approach one per eligible batch.

### F44 — P2: a robust median is neither fresh nor a congestion-tail guarantee

**Evidence:** [timing estimator](../../crates/focal-timing/src/lib.rs), lines 53, 124–159, and 244–265; [retained path estimates](../../crates/focal-wire/src/peers.rs), lines 421–493 and 861–869; [probe cadence](../../crates/focal-node/src/liveness/driver.rs), lines 81 and 780–806; [pace update](../../crates/focal-node/src/network_service.rs), lines 1599–1635.

The 16-sample median/MAD intentionally resists restart/disk outliers. A probe initialized 16 samples at 5 ms and replaced seven with 10 seconds: median stayed 5 ms, advertised tail stayed 6 ms, and default election timeout stayed one second. Some production waits would prevent such slow probes completing; excluded failures are another reason the successful-probe median is not a tail guarantee.

Estimates have no sample-age contract and survive endpoint changes under the same node ID. At a nominal idle 1,024-member, one-selected-probe-per-second round, one member is sampled about every 1,024 seconds. Eight changed samples take approximately 8,192 seconds, or 2 h 16 min, to shift a full-window median. Incidental probes, failures, and dispatch change this arithmetic. Recomputing pace every second does not refresh its evidence.

**Fix:** stamp sample age and endpoint generation; invalidate obsolete paths; prioritize bounded freshness samples for active quorum paths. Keep path delay, durable exchange delay, and local owner/scheduler delay distinct. Track sustained jitter/loss and timeout coverage without stretching all failure detection for a slow learner. Preserve pre-vote, check-quorum, bounded pacing, and truthful recovery guarantees.

**Acceptance:** abrupt LAN-to-WAN change, stable-identity endpoint replacement, burst congestion, asymmetric links, sparse successful probes, slow minority, disk stalls, and scheduler delays at high group/member counts. Report sample age, false campaigns, quorum tails, throughput, and genuine failure-recovery latency.

### F45 — P2: disk receipts already have wakers, but the fleet polls each pending group every millisecond

**Evidence:** [group deadline](../../crates/focal-node/src/fleet.rs), lines 1893–1898; [worker scheduling](../../crates/focal-node/src/fleet_group.rs), lines 395–512; [durability polling](../../crates/focal-consensus/src/persistence.rs), lines 43–47 and 239–248; [wakeable WAL ticket](../../crates/focal-log/src/writer.rs), lines 138–180.

Each pending durability state schedules another 1 ms deadline. Slow storage creates repeated scans proportional to pending groups, and a receipt that just misses a poll waits another scheduling interval. A nominal 1,000 polls/s per pending group is a scheduling calculation, not measured CPU usage; bounded 32-group slices and actual scheduling reduce attainable poll rate while potentially increasing completion latency. `WalAppend` already implements a wakeable future, but the host uses `try_complete`.

**Fix:** send generation-stamped completion hints to the existing worker using a bounded coalesced ready set or a waker tied to its event primitive. The owner must read the actual receipt; a wake is not proof of durability. Retain real tick/shutdown deadlines and a bounded missed-wake fallback. Do not move Raft mutation into the disk thread or create a new task per group.

**Acceptance:** 1/100/1,000 pending groups with a paused or slow WAL. Measure CPU, wakeups, context switches, and receipt-to-owner latency. Cover completion before registration, full notifications, shutdown, restart, and replaced incarnations; prove no lost wake or busy loop.

### F46 — P1: an admitted durable WAL can fail same-budget recovery

**Evidence:** [index charging and construction](../../crates/focal-log/src/writer.rs), lines 237–242, 347–350, 468–469, and 632; [startup frame decoding](../../crates/focal-log/src/lib.rs), line 746.

### F47 — P2: chunked transfer does not make checkpoint installation streaming

**Evidence:** [seed assembly](../../crates/focal-ledger/src/native_checkpoint_read.rs), lines 299–363; [default checkpoint bound](../../crates/focal-ledger/src/native_checkpoint.rs), line 161; [restore and publication](../../crates/focal-ledger/src/native_session_checkpoint.rs), lines 190–272; [row hydration](../../crates/focal-core/src/native/record_codec/recovery.rs), line 219; [dependency phases](../../crates/focal-core/src/native/record_codec/read_index.rs), line 10.

### F48 — P1: native-support discovery cannot traverse a healthy path slower than 250 ms

### F49 — P1: a progressing chunk can arrive after its transfer has expired

**Evidence:** [custody defaults and lease updates](../../crates/focal-node/src/custody.rs), lines 53, 426–429, 470, 520–672, and 901–905; [owner expiry cadence](../../crates/focal-node/src/content_host.rs), lines 170–184; [production chunk size](../../crates/focal-node/src/network_service.rs), line 796.

Transfers expire after 60 seconds by default. The content owner expires them periodically and again before every request lookup; renewal occurs when a decoded custody request is executed. Network body progress does not hold the transfer lease. A production 1 MiB chunk requires **131.1 seconds at 64 kbit/s**, excluding overhead and contention. Its valid first `Chunk` can therefore reach dispatch after the `Open` state has expired and return `Unavailable`, even when the transport has observed useful progress throughout. At this chunk size, 60 seconds alone implies approximately **139.8 kbit/s** useful payload capacity. Durable chunks remain available for resume; the failure is volatile transfer lifetime and completion, not acknowledged content loss.

**Continuation demonstration:** a public `CustodyStore` probe stored chunk 0, injected the owner's expiry at `Instant::now() + 61 seconds`, and sent chunk 1 under the original transfer ID. It returned `Unavailable`, with volatile retained transfer count falling from one to zero. Reopening returned `next_missing=1`, and chunk 1 then stored successfully. This demonstrates expiry/dispatch/resume behavior without waiting 131 seconds or claiming an executed WAN transfer; the bandwidth boundary above remains arithmetic.

**Fix:** tie a funded transfer lease to admitted active chunk progress, with bounded idle/absolute occupancy and cancellation. Support bounded resumable subchunk delivery where fixed immutable chunks exceed a practical transfer interval; preserve the existing chunk hash and exact content identity. Avoid solving slow links by retaining every full chunk indefinitely or simply raising the expiry.

**Acceptance:** push/pull a 1 MiB chunk at 8/64/128 kbit/s, with intermittent outages, expiry concurrent with dispatch, cancellation, and exhausted transfer permits. Useful progress must complete or yield a truthful resumable state; stalled abandoned jobs must reclaim their resources.

### F50 — P2: resume wastes bandwidth on verified chunks beyond the first gap

**Evidence:** [prefix scan and bitmap](../../crates/focal-node/src/custody.rs), lines 575–601 and 624–650; [push range](../../crates/focal-node/src/evidence_service.rs), `push`/`send_chunks`, lines 1250–1330; [pull range](../../crates/focal-node/src/evidence_service.rs), `pull`; [existing-chunk installation](../../crates/focal-evidence/src/store.rs), `install_transferred_chunk` (current lines 1300–1313).

Chunks can arrive out of order. Reopening a transfer scans stored chunks only until the first missing/corrupt chunk, initializes a zeroed receipt bitmap, and returns only `next_missing`. Push/pull then send every chunk from that point to the end. After an interrupted striped transfer, verified chunks beyond an early hole are retransmitted and rehashed. Storage correctly deduplicates an existing verified chunk, but that does not refund network bandwidth. In the worst arrangement, one early gap makes resume resend nearly the complete object.

**Continuation demonstration:** a public custody probe stored chunks 0, 2, and 3 and independently verified the later chunks from disk. Reopen returned `next_missing=1`. After storing the sole physically missing chunk 1, another `Open` reported `next_missing=2`, and `Seal` returned `InvalidRequest` even though all four chunks were now present, totaling 16 payload bytes. The reopened bitmap had forgotten the later verified receipts, so sending only physically missing content cannot finish; retransmission is required. This establishes the state-machine defect and its bandwidth implication, rather than a measured WAN loss trace. F59 records an additional disk-accounting cost of those duplicate sends.

**Fix:** advertise bounded verified missing ranges or a bitmap with continuation, scoped to exact manifest/root/policy. Retain or rebuild a durable verified chunk inventory without turning `Open` into an unbounded whole-object hash pass. Preserve corrupt-chunk repair and validate every claimed skip against trusted local state; keep a compatibility fallback for peers with the older prefix protocol.

**Acceptance:** interrupt/restart after reordered delivery with holes near the beginning/middle, expire volatile transfers, and corrupt a previously present chunk. Count unique missing bytes versus repeated bytes. Resume traffic should be proportional to missing content plus bounded metadata, and corrupt bytes must never be certified by a stale inventory.

### F51 — P2: sealing a large object holds the content actor for a complete verification pass

**Evidence:** [single content owner](../../crates/focal-node/src/content_host.rs), lines 169–195; [seal dispatch](../../crates/focal-node/src/custody.rs), lines 657–672; [complete import](../../crates/focal-evidence/src/transfer.rs), lines 203–259; [chunk installation fences](../../crates/focal-evidence/src/store.rs), `atomic_install` (current lines 1316–1327).

`complete_import()` rereads and hashes all chunks and the whole stream synchronously before installing the manifest. The single actor cannot service other content requests during that call; under default custody admission one object can occupy up to 1 GiB. Resume's prefix scan can similarly perform a long synchronous read/hash pass. Chunk installation also performs individual file and directory fences. Those fences uphold the current durable `ChunkStored` promise; removing them as a speed trick would weaken the protocol. Existing prefix custody verification already advances in bounded slices, so it should be reused as a design precedent rather than alleged absent.

**Fix:** make complete-object verification a retained, charged, generation-scoped state machine with bounded chunk/time slices and fair actor scheduling. Reuse scratch and validated manifest offsets. Evaluate bounded group fencing only with explicit covering receipts: no chunk/manifest/custody success before its required durable fence. Consider source selection by measured health/proximity rather than `ensure_local()`'s serial sorted-peer fallback, with tightly bounded hedging for immutable reads.

**Acceptance:** large seal/resume alongside unrelated reads, chunk import, custody readiness, seed recovery, and owner shutdown. Measure actor wait tails and WAL contention on the shared device. Corruption/cancellation must not publish a durable manifest, and required operations must retain bounded progress.

### F52 — P2: exact allocation still writes every encode buffer twice

**Dated status:** the wire encoder now appends rather than zeroing first. The WAL/fence costs below remain, and the generic append sink's capacity guard is incomplete. Section 19.1 records the partial remedy, compatibility test, and bounded scratch demonstration.

**Evidence:** [wire buffer and encoder](../../crates/focal-wire/src/frame.rs), lines 63–99; [production WAL record encoding](../../crates/focal-log/src/writer.rs), line 624; [fence construction](../../crates/focal-log/src/lib.rs), line 609.

Wire encoding reserves exact capacity, zeroes the complete vector, then overwrites it with Postcard. SharedWal repeats the pattern per record. Exact sizing and byte codecs have removed earlier reallocations, but not this memory-write pass. For large encode traffic these are concrete avoidable write/allocation costs; their CPU/page-fault contribution was not independently profiled.

**Fix:** evaluate a safe bounded fallible append serializer into a reserved empty vector. Receive buffers need a valid initialized-length contract; do not expose uninitialized memory by setting a vector length before initialization. Use existing caller-owned receive seams and bounded size-class/worker pools where measured. Keep permits attached to payload ownership through cancellation; reclaim oversized idle buffers. Retain small fence buffers and paths without changing bytes or sync semantics. Evidence push also clones `Accounted<Vec<u8>>` chunk data into a request; an ownership-preserving move/borrow can remove that copy while retaining its permit.

**Acceptance:** byte-for-byte wire/WAL compatibility, allocation refusal, malformed lengths, initialized-prefix correctness, and cancellation. Measure bytes allocated, copied, and zeroed, not just allocation calls; prove pool RSS falls after churn. Required file/directory fences remain covered.

### F53 — P2: scalar read checks materialize owned collections

**Evidence:** [owned node status](../../crates/focal-consensus/src/lib.rs), line 1203; [readiness comparison](../../crates/focal-ledger/src/session.rs), line 1397; [context construction](../../crates/focal-ledger/src/session.rs), line 1519; [native allocation workload](../../tools/load/benches/allocs.rs).

Transfer correlated contexts without unnecessary cloning, preserving exact identity and publication fences.

**Acceptance:** no membership-vector allocation for scalar checks and no allocation merely to compare a readiness marker. Verify native/legacy linearizable histories through term/configuration changes; attribute barrier, DTO, and transport costs separately.

### F54 — P2: allocator requests, copies, resident memory, and faults were conflated

**Dated status:** counters now separate pointer-moving and in-place reallocations and label requested bytes. Section 19.1 records remaining preserved-length/copy and fault-arithmetic interpretation errors; the historical measurements below retain their original provenance.

**Evidence:** [allocation instrumentation](../../crates/focal-memory/benches/support/alloc_count.rs), lines 134–140; [historical allocation qualification](../qualification/allocations-2026-09-29.md); [Rust realloc contract](https://doc.rust-lang.org/std/alloc/trait.GlobalAlloc.html#method.realloc); host `getconf PAGESIZE`.

`REALLOC_COPIED` adds `min(old, new)` without observing whether realloc remained in place. It is a preservation-size upper bound, **not observed copied bytes**. Cumulative allocation sizes and new realloc sizes are not live memory or RSS. Meter peak/live growth is phase-wide and includes preparation between gated operations. Sampled stacks can be excluded from counters while still perturbing RSS, faults, and timing; even with sampling off, the counting allocator's atomics alter execution. Bounded/dropped samples do not establish an automatic per-site accuracy percentage.

This host's page size is **16,384 bytes**. Historical conversion of fault counts to 4 KiB touched pages is therefore incorrect here. Even the correct page size would not convert fault events into unique newly touched bytes. Zero major faults means zero recorded I/O-requiring faults, not proof of no memory pressure, paging/compression, or reclamation. Counter meanings and RSS units are platform specific. [Apple getrusage](https://developer.apple.com/library/archive/documentation/System/Conceptual/ManPages_iPhoneOS/man2/getrusage.2.html) and [Linux getrusage](https://man7.org/linux/man-pages/man2/getrusage.2.html) document the distinctions.

**Fix:** separate requested, retained, peak, in-place/pointer-moving realloc, actual copy/zero bytes, RSS/footprint, and fault events. Correct the historical interpretations; use count runs for attribution and separate uninstrumented runs for speed/RSS regressions. Record page size, platform, source hash, allocator, build profile, phase boundaries, concurrency, and sampling drops. A pointer move alone also does not reveal the allocator's physical memory-copy traffic.

**Acceptance:** instrumentation fixtures cover in-place realloc, moved allocations, deallocation, peak/reset semantics, and disabled sampling. Qualification reports must label every quantity and avoid substituting allocator requests or fault arithmetic for observed resource use.

### 16.1 Remove amplification before tuning the allocator

Memory admission itself is not the obvious allocator problem: current reserve/commit/refusal operations allocate zero times. Existing funded/elastic pools already reduce reservation work. However, category reclassification still updates shared ancestor atomics. Measure multi-worker cache-line contention before changing counter layout or amortizing telemetry. Strict admission totals must stay correct; approximate per-worker counters cannot silently overcommit a parent cap. Separate exact admission from cheaper batched observational accounting where the contract allows it.

### 16.2 Overlap Raft disk and network without weakening the fence

The local RawNode/Ready contract already separates immediate and persistence-dependent messages. The host currently waits for the WAL receipt before returning both through NodeEvents ([Ready ownership](../../crates/focal-raft/src/node.rs), lines 145–172 and 478; [persistence phases](../../crates/focal-consensus/src/persistence.rs), lines 233–279). Safely overlapping leader replication with its disk write can remove a serial portion of the critical path, especially on WAN links. It is a design candidate, not an implemented or measured gain.

Audit eligibility **by message kind**. The current local split uses leader/follower state rather than a complete per-message dependency classification; blindly emitting every `Ready::messages()` item is insufficient. A funded early-output path may release independently proven leader Append/Heartbeat work only after preflight and retained Ready ownership. Keep term/vote-dependent election messages, follower acknowledgments, application publication, and promised client success behind the covering durable receipt. Advance local durable match only after `advance_append`; preserve the single-outstanding-Ready mutation fence.

Crash-cut every emit/enqueue/write/sync/fence/advance boundary. Verify a restarted leader does not count its lost volatile copy, a follower never acknowledges bytes before persistence, and unknown client outcomes retry exactly. `must_sync=false` is a Raft fact, not blanket permission to weaken Focal's application recovery contract.

SharedWal already group-commits queued work. Next evaluate bounded proposal/Ready batching by bytes and queue age while draining compatible already-admitted requests in one worker slice. Preserve refusal-before-mutation and exact operation identities. A quiet request should proceed immediately; do not purchase high-load throughput with an unbounded or unexplained latency timer. Report quorum time, local fence time, materialization time, and acknowledgment publication separately.

### 16.3 Reduce wasted replication before adding concurrency

Conflict hints already exist, but Probe can construct/send a large suffix before discovering that its preceding prefix is inconsistent. Evaluate a small empty consistency probe for known divergent followers, then send data after a matching acknowledgment. This requires state-machine work: current progress pauses for data-bearing appends, and an unchanged match index does not itself count as advancement. Test delayed/stale probes, divergent terms, compaction, snapshots, and joint configurations. Do not simply swap an empty message into the existing path.

A slow learner should not hold healthy-voter target credits or force the entire fleet to use its timing. Keep bounded per-group/per-recipient data credits, normal versus recovery traffic, and durable progress feedback distinct. Tune application windows to **useful throughput × RTT**, subject to memory and fairness limits. The transport remains responsible for packet congestion control. More simultaneous streams cannot create bandwidth, and maximum stream windows do not need to be resident for every permitted stream.

Freeze ReadIndex batch membership before sending its proof. Preserve a new round for later arrivals, distinct correlated results, and term/configuration/applied-prefix checks. Favor this message amortization before clock leases or speculative follower reads. Optional consensus fast paths remain subject to the wire/WAL/product integration requirements in section 9.

### 16.4 Page faults, locality, and allocator experiments

Logical arena/COW pages are not necessarily OS pages. First reduce full-frame zeroing, deep copies of touched hot namespaces, oversized staging, index fragmentation, and old/new/input coexistence. Those create unnecessary writes and working-set expansion that allocator substitution cannot remove.

Then compare ordinary allocations with bounded size-class worker pools and supported allocator alternatives using **uninstrumented** release workloads. Include short-lived/cross-thread allocations, many cold groups, peak-frame churn, cancellation, recovery, and near-limit memory. Observe fragmentation, private/file-backed residency, allocator retained memory, and reclamation after work ends. A lower allocation count with permanently retained maximum-sized buffers is a failed resource optimization.

For Linux fleet hosts, measure NUMA placement, remote accesses, cache/TLB misses, scheduler migration, allocator arenas, and cgroup memory pressure. Test selective prefaulting or huge-page advice only for dense, stable hot regions. [Linux's THP documentation](https://docs.kernel.org/admin-guide/mm/transhuge.html) describes reduced TLB pressure alongside larger clearing/compaction and footprint costs. Do not blanket-enable huge pages, prefault the maximum heap, or lock memory on laptops and pressured nodes; fewer faults alone is not a useful result. A mapped immutable checkpoint can reduce heap copying while increasing file-backed fault/reclaim sensitivity. Qualify that tradeoff under cold-cache restore and memory pressure.

Track Linux minor/major faults and RSS/smaps/cgroup peaks separately from macOS page reclaims/faults/footprint and Windows working-set counters. Record actual page size. Include process and kernel/socket memory: Focal's logical memory budget does not by itself account for every transport/kernel buffer or allocator-retained page. Attribute fault time and copy/zero bandwidth to useful committed work, not only totals.

### F55 — P1: legitimate follower read lag becomes a failed session

**Evidence:** [session delivery](../../crates/focal-ledger/src/session.rs), lines 1397–1401; [native correlated reads](../../crates/focal-ledger/src/native_session_apply.rs), lines 861–867; [follower ReadIndex response](../../crates/focal-raft/src/raft.rs), lines 1771–1790.

The Raft follower correctly accepts a leader's `MsgReadIndexResp` and emits its read state even if the matching log entry has not arrived locally. QUIC streams and separate exchanges need not deliver the response after every preceding append. The host then requires `barrier.index <= applied_raft` immediately; otherwise it returns `Corrupt`. This is legitimate replication lag, not evidence of invalid committed state.

A real `Session` probe opened follower 2 in a three-voter configuration, learned leader 1 in term 1 while applied index was zero, and requested a read. Its forwarded `MsgReadIndex` was observed. Receiving the matching leader read response at index 1 before the missing append caused `poll()` to return **`Err(Corrupt)`**, then **`Err(Failed)`** on the next poll. This proves owner failure at the shared session boundary. The native correlated-read guard has the same immediate-lag assumption; a full native multi-node reproduction was not run. No incorrect read or committed-data loss was established.

**Fix:** retain read barriers in a separately bounded, funded pending set and complete them only after the local applied index reaches their boundary. Continue accepting the log traffic needed for catch-up. Merely retaining the entire current delivery until the read is ready can block the later delivery containing the missing append. Preserve context identity, incarnation/term handling, cancellation, and output capacity. [Etcd's Ready/ReadStates contract](https://github.com/etcd-io/raft/blob/main/node.go) likewise requires the application to wait for its applied index to reach the returned boundary before serving the read.

**Acceptance:** force read responses before append delivery, during asynchronous WAL fences and native materialization, and across leader/configuration changes. Reads wait or return truthful bounded unavailability; the owner remains live and catches up. Assert no read serves below its confirmed barrier and no retained barrier prevents append progress.

### F56 — P1: ordinary waiters consume the admission needed for their completion

**Evidence:** [Raft ingress and pending queue](../../crates/focal-node/src/fleet.rs), lines 2303–2323 and 2989–2996; default `pending_clients=128`, line 132.

Authenticated Raft ingress checks `self.pending.len() == pending_clients` before `step_authenticated()`. Participant reads/mutations and `PeerPersistence` acknowledgments share this pending deque. Once participant work fills it, even a valid tiny heartbeat/append response needed to establish quorum progress is refused with `Capacity`. Transport probe lanes and completion memory reserve cannot override this earlier item-admission dependency. Pending work may eventually time out/cancel and free capacity, but doing so is not healthy-quorum progress under admitted load.

A public `ReplicaHost` probe used a real session/WAL, a ready three-voter leader, and a one-slot pending configuration. An authenticated participant summary occupied that slot and emitted a ReadIndex heartbeat. The matching response from admitted voter 2 was dispatched through normal authentication/admission and returned **`Error(Capacity)`**; the participant remained pending. Reducing the bound to one isolates the same equality check used at 128; a 128-client load campaign was not executed. This is ingress starvation, independent of F42's egress scheduling problem.

**Fix:** separate and fund bounded Raft completion admission from ordinary participant waiters. Reserve usable item/byte headroom for authenticated progress traffic, not only transport permits. Cover generated messages and the ingress success with the exact required durability fence; do not acknowledge before persistence or discard completion obligations. Fair scheduling must still prevent repeated peer traffic from starving participants.

**Acceptance:** fill the actual 128 participant slots while delivering healthy quorum acknowledgments, votes, read proofs, learner traffic, and membership responses. Commit/read progress must continue before participant timeout. Repeat with paused WAL, cancellation, full ordinary memory credit, and joint configurations; verify accounting and acknowledgment fences.

### F57 — P1: recovery's cumulative work envelope is smaller than admitted history

**Evidence:** [production native limits](../../crates/focal-ledger/src/native_session.rs), lines 134–145 and 186–191; [checkpoint limits/encoding](../../crates/focal-ledger/src/native_checkpoint.rs), lines 168–175 and 284–287; [event admission](../../crates/focal-core/src/native/prepare.rs), lines 1496–1501; [recovery validation meter](../../crates/focal-core/src/native/record_codec/recovery.rs), lines 256–266; [history-index construction](../../crates/focal-core/src/native/record_codec/read_history.rs), lines 115–127 and 172–181; [lookup work](../../crates/focal-core/src/native/record_codec/read_validate.rs), lines 525–537.

Admission bounds events/rows/bytes, while recovery uses independently cumulative parsing/source/model/lookup allowances. Final structural/model validation shares the model allowance already used during hydration. History-index construction precharges heapsort for capacity `2 * meta.events`, including unused secondary-index allowance, rather than the actual populated index count. The complete restore work envelope is not checked when admitting the history or creating its checkpoint. This is separate from F02's explicit outcome-count mismatch and F46's SharedWal index expansion.

A release Core probe used the production `NativeLimits`, ProjectionOnly content, a 128 MiB budget, and 4,096 valid claim creations in 256 batches of 16. Its charged live state was **28,088,728 bytes**, with **8,192 events and 45,313 rows**. Checkpoint quotation reported **6,156,806 bytes and 207,812,775 encoding visits**, fitting the default **100,000 rows, 256 MiB assembled root, and 268,435,456 encoding visits**. The root exceeds the four-MiB inline bound and is valid for seeded checkpoint encoding, which uses the assembled bound. Restore with the production **`model=1 << 30`** work allowance returned **`Contract(Capacity)`**. Changing only that diagnostic allowance to `2 << 30` restored prefix 256 successfully. A 2,048-claim control checkpoint restored under the original allowance.

The 8,192-event history-sort precharge alone is **268,435,456 model units**, before hydration and other validation; it is not the entire measured restore cost. This is a demonstrated Core checkpoint incompatibility under production limits, not an executed Session process restart or proof of lost WAL. Doubling the diagnostic limit establishes the cause; it is not the recommended fix.

**Fix:** make every admitted/checkpointed state fit the complete supported recovery envelope. Charge actual index population, avoid redundant full-state passes where an equivalent bounded proof exists, and define history retirement/partitioning before the envelope is exhausted. Preserve corruption detection and decoder floors. Required checkpoint/recovery work should be sliceable and independently funded; failure after durable publication cannot be the normal capacity policy.

**Acceptance:** create/checkpoint/restore continuous native workloads at production limits, including empty/populated secondary histories, cancellation/release/retirement, all profiles, multiple ranges, and crash cuts. Same-configuration restore must succeed for admitted durable states. Record parsing/source/model/lookup consumption separately and test boundaries immediately before/after each limit.

### F58 — P2: completion-funded restore encounters ordinary-only constructors

**Evidence:** [recovery index](../../crates/focal-core/src/native/record_codec/read_index.rs), lines 122–131; [range constructor](../../crates/focal-memory/src/range.rs), lines 397–400; [phased hydration initialization](../../crates/focal-memory/src/range_hydration_phased.rs), lines 118–120; [checkpoint layout](../../crates/focal-core/src/native/record_codec/checkpoint.rs), lines 316–321; [range assembly funding](../../crates/focal-core/src/native/ranges.rs), lines 196–203 and 270–285; [production install call](../../crates/focal-ledger/src/native_session_checkpoint.rs), lines 262–268.

Recovery uses completion-class work in several stages, but initial `RangeStore` roots, decoded layout, and assembled collection funding still hardcode the ordinary lane. An assembly API accepts a lane yet calls a helper that reserves ordinary root bytes. The completion reserve therefore does not guarantee that already-required restore can even initialize under ordinary pressure.

A 16-claim checkpoint was **24,311 bytes/178 rows** and normally restored with **111,136 charged bytes**. Holding the ordinary 112 MiB allowance of a 128 MiB budget as an accounting reservation, while all **16 MiB completion credit remained free**, made restore fail at **`Memory(Capacity { requested: 224, available: 0 })`**. Releasing the reservation restored the identical checkpoint successfully. An empty 8 MiB completion-funded child budget instead returned **`InvalidConfiguration("completion funding cannot admit ordinary work")`**. These are Core funding probes; the reservation does not represent 112 MiB of actual resident payload, and follower installation under live load was not benchmarked.

**Fix:** propagate the intended lane through root/index/layout/hydration/assembly initialization and retain permits through publication/cancellation. Keep completion funding restricted to genuine completion/repair; granting ordinary producers access would defeat the reserve. Budget old state, input, scratch, and new roots together rather than relying on nominal free reserve alone.

**Acceptance:** restore/install admitted checkpoints with ordinary credit exhausted and sufficient specifically reserved recovery credit. Verify cancellation and error cleanup, typed refusal when the actual recovery reserve is insufficient, and conservation across child budgets; ordinary work must remain unable to borrow completion-only funding.

### F59 — P2: exact duplicate chunk retries falsely consume the disk estimate

**Evidence:** [import reservation](../../crates/focal-evidence/src/transfer.rs), lines 181–194; [verified existing chunk](../../crates/focal-evidence/src/store.rs), `install_transferred_chunk`; [committed disk estimate](../../crates/focal-memory/src/disk.rs), lines 268–280 and 315–317; resampling at lines 133–144 and 207–216.

Import reserves the entire chunk payload and unconditionally commits that reservation. The installer correctly recognizes an identical existing chunk, verifies it, and refreshes its freshness metadata without rewriting the payload. Committing the reservation still subtracts its size from estimated free disk space. Exact retries and F50's forced retransmissions can therefore report disk pressure even though payload occupancy has not increased. Physical observation can correct the estimate; normal nonzero-headroom/admission triggers limit drift, but there is no timer-guaranteed resampling. In the controlled zero-headroom case, refused requests do not advance the sampling counter, so false refusal persists until another observation. This is not demonstrated permanent exhaustion under production defaults, nor a claim that freshness updates perform no metadata I/O.

A controlled public store/DiskBudget probe observed 16 free bytes, with zero headroom and the default 32-admission sample interval. An existing verified four-byte chunk accepted four duplicate imports; estimated free bytes fell **16 → 0**, while the chunk file stayed four bytes. The fifth returned **`Capacity`**, with `sample_due=false`. Refreshing the same free-space estimate made the retry succeed and the estimate fall to 12 again. The tiny observation isolates accounting near a watermark; it does not measure allocated filesystem blocks or a physical full-disk condition.

**Fix:** let installation report whether new/repair payload bytes were actually installed and shrink/drop the payload reservation on verified deduplication. Account independently for any real metadata/staging cost. Preserve freshness needed by GC and corruption repair; inspect duplicate manifest/record installation for the same unconditional-commit pattern.

**Acceptance:** repeated exact chunk retries and reordered resume near the disk watermark must not invent cumulative payload use. New and corrupt/repaired chunks retain correct admission. Resampling, failure before/after rename/sync, and simultaneous reservations conserve estimates and durable receipts.

### F60 — P2: cold participant calls dial independently and replace one another

**Evidence:** [participant QUIC cache](../../crates/focal-client/src/transport.rs), lines 132–174 and 180–191; [identity replacement](../../crates/focal-wire/src/admission.rs), connection-admission and replacement handling. This is the participant `QuicTransport`, not the node peer pool, which already coalesces dials.

The cache mutex protects lookup/insertion but is released before DNS and connect. Concurrent cold misses for the same endpoint each dial, then overwrite the same entry while retaining their individual `QuicRemote`. `max_connections` bounds cached route keys, not simultaneous physical connections or in-progress dials. The server's per-principal identity limit correctly replaces old connections, which can abort already-dispatched ordinary requests. A later error also removes the route key without checking whether a newer healthy connection replaced the failed one; that additional cache-generation problem is source-backed.

A real loopback QUIC probe issued **24 concurrent ordinary Actor calls** to one cold endpoint with cache limit one. It observed **24 admitted connections**, **16 currently tracked connections**, **8 replacements**, and **16/24 completed calls** after releasing held replies. All 24 handlers started in the first run; an independent rerun reproduced the connection/completion counts with only 16 handler starts, because some replacements preceded dispatch. Admission/handler-start counts are cumulative. The cancellations occur through identity replacement; the first run completed without waiting for its observation deadline. These counts do not establish a CPU/RSS speedup or a global connection-cap violation; they establish uncoalesced dial churn and canceled calls, including already-dispatched calls in the first run.

**Fix:** share one bounded, cancellation-safe pending dial per route/generation, with bounded waiter admission and explicit physical-connection ownership. Remove cached connections only when the failed generation is still current. Preserve parallelism across different endpoints and safe route/certificate retirement; copying the node pool's ownership design is preferable to serializing every client request under one global mutex.

**Acceptance:** cold bursts, route changes, reconnect after outage, initiator/waiter cancellation, and delayed old-connection failures. Count dials, replacements, canceled admitted calls, handshake bytes, and peak memory. A future regression must release handler gates for every wave: waiting for 24 simultaneous handlers would itself deadlock after the fix legitimately shares one 16-stream connection.

### F61 — P2: cursor renewals copy unrelated consumers and idle polls persist work

**Evidence:** [registry preparation](../../crates/focal-stream/src/registry.rs), lines 229–284; [poll renewal/proposal](../../crates/focal-node/src/streams.rs), lines 250–287 and 410–437; [CLI polling interval](../../crates/focal-node/src/cli/watch.rs), lines 161–184; [session cursor integration](../../crates/focal-ledger/src/cursor_session.rs) and [managed integration](../../crates/focal-ledger/src/managed_session.rs).

Renewing one consumer reserves `checkpoint_charge(old)`, deep-clones the entire consumer map and each filter, then validates the whole checkpoint. This repeats for a scalar lease change. Stream polls first establish ReadIndex and then propose `Renew` or `AcknowledgeAndRenew`, including polls that deliver no new domain events. CLI follow sleeps only 250 ms after each delivered page before the next request; durable client acknowledgment/retry bookkeeping also occurs for empty deliveries. A fleet of idle observers therefore drives consensus, WAL, registry copying, and client disk work unrelated to useful event progress.

A gated allocator probe measured only preparation of one renewal, with fixture construction excluded:

| Registry rows | Filter claims per row | Allocation/reallocation calls | Cumulative requested bytes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0 | 1 | 2,304 |
| 32 | 0 | 5 | 11,616 |
| 256 | 0 | 42 | 97,344 |
| 1,024 | 0 | 171 | 396,384 |
| 64 | 256 | 1,610 | 348,768 |

These are measured preparation requests, not live memory/RSS or time per watch poll. The fleet implication is source-backed: if C consumers each renew at rate R and the registry contains C rows, aggregate full-row copy work grows as **O(C²R)** before filters and consensus overhead. This is distinct from F18's native namespace copying and F43's repeated quorum-proof broadcasts.

**Fix:** use incremental/persistent consumer updates with narrow validation and bounded filter sharing; avoid full-registry copying for renewal/acknowledgment. Introduce funded bounded long polling or progress notifications, and batch/pace lease renewal within its actual expiration contract. Preserve durable acknowledgments before releasing retention/effect obligations and exact retry behavior; replacing explicit acknowledgment with an optimistic in-memory cursor is not an equivalent optimization.

**Acceptance:** 1/100/1,000 consumers, empty/busy ledgers, maximum filters, expired/seeding/protected consumers, retries, slow readers, and leader changes. Record allocations/bytes, useful events per commit, WAL bytes/syncs, idle CPU, and retention correctness. Idle fleets should not incur a full-registry copy and durable renewal every 250 ms per consumer.

### F62 — P1: expired consumer rows never return their admission slots

**Evidence:** [consumer admission](../../crates/focal-stream/src/registry.rs), lines 324–326 and 412–414; [floor advancement](../../crates/focal-stream/src/registry.rs), lines 472–486; [retention eligibility](../../crates/focal-stream/src/registry.rs), lines 554–565; default `max_consumers=4096`, lines 110–116; `CursorOperation` has no retire/remove operation.

Expiry/resync releases an ordinary consumer's retention constraint, but the row remains in `consumers`. Admission compares the complete map length, including expired/resynced names. Advancing the retention floor does not remove them. Reusing the same consumer through `BeginSeed` advances its generation, but new identities cannot reuse those slots. Continuous creation of distinct consumers therefore reaches a **cumulative 4,096-name ceiling per registry**, even with few or no live observers. This is a lifecycle/capacity defect for continuous operation, not a reason to raise the limit; protected consumers must retain their distinct durable obligations.

A public registry probe with capacity one registered a consumer expiring at time 1, advanced the floor to published prefix 1 at time 2, and tried to register a different valid consumer at time 3. There were **zero live consumers, one retained row, and floor 1**; admission still returned **`Capacity`**. The test isolates the default comparison rather than executing 4,097 real clients. Metadata owner/receipt state requires coordinated lifecycle review as well; deleting only the map row would be incomplete.

**Fix:** define durable consumer retirement and compacted generation/incarnation fences that make slots reclaimable without letting stale requests resurrect an old ownership scope. Coordinate registry, owner metadata, receipts, checkpoints, and replay. Preserve protected consumers until their explicit proof/effect obligation is satisfied; expiry alone must not erase them.

**Acceptance:** churn more than 4,096 distinct ordinary consumers while maintaining a small live set, then checkpoint/restart/reseed. State and metadata remain bounded and new consumers remain admissible. Stale acknowledgments/renewals/old exact retries cannot mutate or regain ownership of a reclaimed generation; protected retention remains intact.

### F63 — P2: identical contexts from different readers lose one response

**Evidence:** [ReadOnly deduplication](../../crates/focal-raft/src/read.rs), lines 55–88; [read reply destination](../../crates/focal-raft/src/raft.rs), `answer_read`; [native correlation](../../crates/focal-node/src/native_reads.rs), lines 105–117; [owner nonce/context](../../crates/focal-node/src/fleet.rs), lines 2922–2926.

`ReadOnly::add` keys only on context bytes and drops a duplicate without retaining its `from` origin. Native correlation hashes principal, request ID, and an owner-local nonce; node/owner incarnation is omitted. The same principal's exact read retry at two followers with aligned nonces can therefore produce the same context deterministically. The leader retains only the first destination, leaving the other reader without its quorum result.

A public RawNode probe submitted the same context from distinct follower origins 2 and 3. The leader reported **one pending read**, and after quorum confirmation emitted a read response only to **destination `[2]`**. This demonstrates dropped reader origin in the core. The native correlation construction establishes a concrete collision route; an end-to-end native routed-retry reproduction was not run. No incorrect read value or quorum bypass was established.

**Fix:** scope contexts to node and owner incarnation, or retain a bounded set of distinct origins/waiters when coalescing an identical request. Keep batching and exact retry identity explicit; a client request ID is not itself a globally unique per-owner read-round identifier. [Etcd's read-only implementation](https://github.com/etcd-io/raft/blob/main/read_only.go) documents unique request context as a requirement; its current implementation also differs in read-round construction, so it is a contract reference rather than a drop-in patch.

**Acceptance:** same principal/request at multiple followers, owner restart with nonce reset, leader change, cancellation, and concurrent reads with colliding externally supplied contexts. Every retained origin gets a safe boundary or explicit bounded refusal; coalescing cannot silently strand another reader.

### F64 — P2: backoff alone does not spread a recovery wave

**Evidence:** [client retry policy and loop](../../crates/focal-client/src/client.rs), lines 15–27 and 706–714; [peer retry/cooldown](../../crates/focal-wire/src/peers.rs), lines 58–59, 839, and 894.

The participant loop uses deterministic capped exponential sleeps: default 20, 40, 80, …, 500 ms, without jitter. Attempt/refusal/elapsed limits are bounded and should be retained. Clients affected together by a leader outage, reconnect, or shared capacity refusal can keep offering aligned retry waves. Default availability refusals allow up to 64 backed-off resends, subject to the 30-second envelope, and can reach repeated 500 ms sleeps; capacity refusals have only three resends, and ordinary transport failures have four attempts. The node pool's fixed retry delay/cooldown also provides no fleet-level spreading. Real RTT variance may spread some calls; the absence of jitter and the synchronization route are source-backed, not a measured thousand-client storm.

**Fix:** use bounded full/decorrelated jitter under the existing elapsed/attempt/refusal envelope, with independently seeded callers and deterministic injectable randomness for testing. Keep exact mutation request identity stable. Consider a funded retry token allowance and structured server pressure hints rather than multiplying retry layers. [AWS's primary backoff analysis](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/architecture/exponential-backoff-and-jitter/) shows why deterministic capped backoff preserves clusters of calls and compares jittered alternatives; its numerical results are not claimed for Focal.

**Acceptance:** synchronized fail/recovery with thousands of clients, low-bandwidth reverse paths, full pending queues, and connection churn. Measure total attempts, useful completions, refusal tails, fairness, and recovery CPU/handshakes. Do not obtain a better success rate by increasing offered retry work or extending the existing promise indefinitely.

### F65 — P2: a diagnostics wait delays every later metric and publication

**Evidence:** [fleet collection](../../crates/focal-node/src/network_service.rs), lines 1230–1245 and 1582–1587; [owner diagnostics wait](../../crates/focal-node/src/fleet_diagnostics.rs), lines 19–36; [loopback rendering](../../crates/focal-node/src/metrics.rs), lines 959–1000; five-second sample interval at line 17.

Metrics collection awaits each session diagnostic sequentially, then publishes the completed snapshot and sleeps five seconds. `diagnostics()` bounds channel admission and charges 64 KiB, but waits on the accepted reply without a deadline. A busy/stuck owner can delay all later sessions and leave the previous snapshot published; collection time also extends the nominal five-second cadence. The sample timestamp is exposed, so consumers can detect age, but collection has no fair partial-publication/global work budget. This is distinct from F26's permanent first-512 visibility gap. No 512-session daemon staleness experiment was executed.

The serial loopback HTTP server renders the complete current snapshot before reading/validating the request, outside its two-second request timeout, and repeats rendering for every scrape—even a subsequent 404/405. Bounded snapshot size limits the work, but repeated concurrent scrapers or one slow client can delay others. This is local observability cost, not a remote authorization finding.

**Fix:** collect through bounded fair concurrency with explicit collection deadline/work funding, publish age/error/partial coverage, and preserve unknown status on failed observations. Rotate bounded details while maintaining fleet aggregates. Render once per published generation or only after validating a request; keep bounded client concurrency and cleanup. Health serving must remain inexpensive and independent of expensive per-owner diagnostics. Count collection failures rather than silently treating skipped groups as healthy.

**Acceptance:** blocked/busy/disconnected owner amid a large fleet, slow scrapers, repeated invalid requests, and tight completion memory. Healthy groups' measurements continue to publish within the stated freshness envelope; age and missing coverage remain visible. Measure sampler/render allocations, CPU, queue occupancy, and scrape tails.
