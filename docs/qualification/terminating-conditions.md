(entries of [09-implementation-status](../archictecutre/09-implementation-status.md), cited by date
and title, the tests they name, and the CI runs they measured) and what that evidence does not yet
show. A condition is met only when its open column is empty. Nothing here is restated from memory:
every row names where it is recorded, and a gap is written as a gap.

## 1. One binary and CLI, laptop to global

**Recorded.** The product is one executable, `focal`: the node, the CLI, the MCP adapter and
the diagnostics (`focal diagnose …`); the measurement tools (`focal-load`, `focal-compare`) are
separate and ship with none of it; the grammar is `focal ACTION THING` ([manual-cli](../manual-cli.md)).
The [deployment complexity study](deployment-complexity.md) is generated byte for byte from the
real binary's journey tests, stage by stage from a laptop (`deployment_laptop.rs`) through zones,
regions, a fleet, Kubernetes and upgrades (`deployment_*.rs`), each stage failing until the study
records the operator concepts it introduces. The [capacity envelope](capacity-envelope.md) labels
every figure measured, extrapolated or unmeasured.

**Open.** "Global" rests on the regions journey and the envelope; no multi-region deployment
has been measured under load.

## 2. A thousand agents and more

**Recorded.** "2026-10-22 — A thousand agents do the work among themselves": focal-load's `work`
lifecycle (a claim posted, taken, its artifact submitted, a testament submitted and posted,
received, its whole-work validation entered, begun and reported, the claim settled and released:
eleven writes) among 1,000 enrolled agents, each on a connection of its own, three 1-CPU nodes
(CI 39870118553): 1,200 and 3,000 claims satisfied in the measured minute at 20 and 50 claims a
second, none refused and none unknown; satisfied p50 47 and 128 ms, p99 158 and 622 ms. The
listener admits what the node's memory funds ("2026-10-20 — A node's listener admits what its
memory funds: a thousand agents and more").

**Open.** Rates past 50 claims a second among a thousand agents; the condition's application, a
Kubernetes implementation in Rust built by the agents, has not been attempted.

## 3. Delivery under load, horrific networks and failures

**Recorded.** Work among 500 agents over netem paths: 80 ms ± 30 ms, 3% loss, and both with 5%
reordering, every write committed, none refused or unknown (compare.yml on load-reads 09c3b89e, runs
39973449354, 39973458138, 39973468524; "2026-10-23 — focal-load reads as many of a caller's
lifecycle steps at once as it writes"); under 80 ms and 3% loss on every leg a write's median 1.18
times the loss-free path's (293 against 249 ms), every lifecycle written ("2026-10-28 — A write
under 80 ms and 3% loss: replication's head-of-line wait, rebuilt by forward erasure correction");
QUIC learning a reordering path's tolerance ("2026-10-23 — QUIC tolerates a path that reorders"); a
content copy holder killed for 30 s and isolated for 20 s ("2026-10-23 — An artifact job does not
ask a copy the liveness view holds down" and the entries after it: isolated, every write committed;
killed, 88 refused and 10 unknown of 12,514 at the clients' 30 s clocks); 128 kbit/s relays (the
F48/F49 and MTU-probe entries); tests run beside busy loops and small memory (the CLI tests' starts,
"2026-10-23 — A test's wait for a started node is charged to the node's periods").

A write sent again committed a median of 25.9 to 32.0 s after its first send in k34 (sessions
without their quorum until their voters served again, and custody), 46.4 to 67.0 s in m9 (its
twelve down 66.4 s, artifacts waiting for their copies), 9.8 s in c19 and 24.2 s in p11; none ran
past the wait. A write's latency now carries its wait, so these p99s are not comparable with the
runs above. No write was refused `ingress_capacity`, and every window in which a session committed
nothing, read against every node's log I/O thread and the volume's commits sampled every 200 ms
(k34, m9, c19), was a session without its quorum, an election, artifacts waiting on a copy, or a
move's cutover.

The directory healed every session it could, two of them while the twelve were still down (m6;
none in m5 before 01a2f923). No outcome stayed unknown and no lifecycle was abandoned where the
fault ended within the run. m7's were all in sessions without a quorum to the run's end. A session
without its quorum committed again 37 to 70 s after the kill, as its voters came back. The
confirmation found two more defects and fixed them at the cause: a move's session was closed to its
clients longer the later it came in ledger order (5.2 to 19.6 s, b9f8a514), and the root's owner
synced every node-local intent journal itself. That stalled the root group for up to 20 s in a
bootstrap, which is p9-0639's stop (832a0567). In m8 and k30 the owners' syncs were gone, and the
move windows were 5.1 to 8.4 s.

**Open.** An artifact's acknowledgement waits on every content copy of its session (doc 26 §1), so
while a copy is down its artifact steps wait or are refused (`unavailable` at the client's 30 s
clock, `owner_bound` at the custody check): every refusal at 33 nodes that is not a session without
its quorum is this rule's. With agents that retry, every artifact committed under it: the rule
sets how long an artifact waits while a copy is down (median 31.3 s in k34, 50.6 s in m9), not
whether it completes, and whether a quorum of copies (f+1) suffices is the user's decision,
pending. A session whose quorum is down takes no write until its voters are back. A move closes its
session to its clients from its log's cutover to its activation, 5.1 to 8.4 s in m8. `ingress_capacity`
(k28 49, k29 40, c18 12) is a session's door full while its voters' syncs are held, root-caused in
c18: no session committed from 11.7 to 13.0 s after the cut began, and the log I/O threads of the
same sixteen live nodes sat in disk waits in every 200 ms sample from 11.8 to 12.8 s, the one btrfs
volume every node's log is on stalled; the session that had lost a follower, its two live voters
holding every commit and 231 writes under way, refused its next 12 once the client's three capacity
resends were spent. k28's and k29's were each one such two-voter session, which a voter's held
sync fits (no thread samples then). A heal outlives its cause: a move runs to its activation after
its voter is alive again (c18's focal33, never cut off, held dead about 2.2 s, a session that lost
nothing then closed 6.5 s; two of m9's three heals cut over after their voter was back), and a heal
withdrawn before its cutover is not done. A node's log syncs wait on its file
system's commits, up to 5.2 s on the host's btrfs volume, where the harness keeps every node's log
(condition 10). Under 3% loss a write's p99 is 753
to 2,244 ms against 462 to 574 without, its artifact steps held at the evidence door (the 2026-10-28
entry's open items).

## 5. Post-quantum encryption in transit and at rest

**Recorded.** In transit: "2026-10-04 — F58: every connection exchanges its keys post-quantum"
(X25519MLKEM768 on every provider focal builds, through aws-lc-rs). At rest (doc 29 §12): the node
log, the group files, the payload log (per-record AES-GCM), every data directory's keys and the
enrollment secrets are sealed; a sealed record is opened only when read. The content store's chunks
and manifests are sealed under a key per domain and named by keyed hashes from the content level
(level 9; doc 29 §12.4, "2026-10-23 — The content store seals its objects from the content level"),
a store in the clear converted while it serves. A session's checkpoint seeds are sealed under a key
per seed directory and named by keyed hashes from the at-rest level (level 10; doc 29 §12.5,
"2026-10-24 — A session's checkpoint seeds are sealed from the at-rest level"), a directory in the
clear converted while it serves by jobs off the session's owner. A session's cold streams are sealed
from the same level (doc 29 §12.6, "2026-10-24 — A session's cold streams are sealed from the
at-rest level"): the open chunk record by record under a key of its file's own, a whole chunk the
same file renamed under a keyed name, a manifest sealed keyed by its hash, a directory in the clear
converted while the stream goes on. A content store's uploads being staged, chunks being received
in parts and custody records are sealed from the same level (doc 29 §12.7, "2026-10-24 — A content
store's staging, parts and custody records are sealed from the at-rest level"), apart from its
objects' form, so a node between the content and at-rest levels still rolls back. A session's
backup is sealed from the same level under the operator's key file (doc 29 §12.8, "2026-10-24 — A
session's backups are sealed under the operator's key file"): a key made once for the backup,
wrapped in its `ENCRYPTION-INFO`, every other file sealed whole under it with keyed names. A bus
node's part image and run are sealed under its data directory's keys, and the bus's identity
directory under keys of its own, each key file a sealed frame of its name and key (doc 29 §12.9,
"2026-10-25 — The bus's images, runs and identity keys are sealed"), each converted at its next open
and refused in the clear after its mark. A node's small files (its identity, policy, network state,
founded allowance, partition records, deployment plans and journals, pending retirements, and the
issuers its client enrollments adopted) are sealed whole from the at-rest level, each its name
bound inside its seal (doc 29 §12.10, "2026-10-25 — A node's small files are sealed from the at-rest
level"), converted at its start before it serves. A client's stores (its requests and their
receipts, its pending writes, its managed ownership, its watches and its uploads' staged bytes) are
sealed from the same level (doc 29 §12.11, "2026-10-25 — A client's stores are sealed from the
at-rest level"): in a node's data directory behind the node's fence, in a client's own behind the
fence it learns from its cluster (`Operation::Fence`), each store converted at its next open with
every pending request answered once. The whole of it is held by one journey on the real binary
(doc 29 §12.12, "2026-10-26 — Every store focal writes is sealed at rest, held end to end"): a
cluster founded below the level with every store populated, the fence raised, every store converted
while it serves, and every data directory scanned for what the journey wrote and for every store's
clear form, run again and again on one CPU and on two beside busy loops.

**Open.** None: every store of doc 29 §12's inventory is sealed from its level, and every file
left in the clear is listed there clear by design, with its reason.

## 6. Adversarial batteries

**Recorded.** "2026-10-05 — Every YAML document under a stated budget, held by a contract rule"
(alias-expansion bombs refused; `check-contracts.py` refuses the default budget in production);
focal compresses nothing, and its wire, record and input codecs refuse a declared length before
allocating (R11 §3); tampering with sealed records and acknowledged frames is refused and reported
(the seal and log entries; hyper-log's `damage_to_any_field_of_an_acknowledged_frame_is_reported`).

**Open.** One battery that runs every adversarial case together, and a statement of its coverage
against a threat list, does not exist yet.

## 7. Meta scale by default

**Recorded.** The [capacity envelope](capacity-envelope.md) states fleet scaling and global scale
as an envelope from measured per-session and per-node figures, never as a demonstrated result; doc
31 states the message-ledger direction (sharded, in memory first, durability in the background).

**Open.** No fleet beyond a few nodes has been measured.
