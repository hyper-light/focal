832a0567 AL: a node's local intent journals are written on a writer thread of their own, never by a control owner: every placement intent's journal write (and the network controller's) went to the root's control owner, which wrote the journal whole and synced its file and its directory itself. On fedora's btrfs volume the root owner was seen in `fsync` for as long as the volume took (fleet33 k29, 2026-11-02, sampled every 200 ms inside the nodes: state D in fsync on `placement-partition/.pending-…` and on the directory, in `wait_log_commit`, `folio_wait_bit_common` and `barrier_all_devices`), and meanwhile the root replica took no tick, sent no heartbeat and stepped no frame. In m7's bootstrap the founder's and focal3's root owners stalled together for 5.9 s and for 20 s with no checkpoint under way, and the root elected another leader as each stall ended, the shape of p9-0639's stop. Intents queued behind the owner's work besides, refused `Capacity` when its queue was full. Each control host now starts a journal writer (`focal-journal-<node>`): admitted writes are made in order, bounded as the owner's queue is, each behind an unwind fence; a write not admitted (queue full or writer gone) hands the journal back whole, as before. The directory partition's owner gets its own. `a_local_intent_is_written_without_its_control_owner` stops a root owner and saves an intent through its host; without this it fails `Unavailable` (checked on fedora)
