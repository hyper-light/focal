# The work flow on 33 nodes: every session a ledger, every lifecycle judged (goal condition 3 at scale)

Date: 2026-10-29. focal at `fleet-run`: the 30-node plan's first four steps (`harness-nodes`,
`harness-sessions`, `load-ledgers`, `harness-sizing`) merged on `bus-replicated` 9aded7cd, with
this step's harness: f0989e17 in runs r2 and r3, 71fc3392 (its disk and rest records) in r4, and
e2f8152f after them (the bench session's replicas read only where a node holds it, checked by
v5-1028 below). Each ran from a copy of the tree with its change not yet committed (`tree.txt`),
byte for byte the commit's files. The binaries are the same in every run: built once on the host as
`focal:fleet` from `tools/compare/compose/focal.Dockerfile` at 3723c4bc, whose Rust is the
branch's (the harness commits change only `tools/compare`), with a cargo target cache of its own.

**Host:** fedora, the host step 4 chose (kiln runner kiln-a094a12d.attlocal.net, hostname
fedora-2): Intel Core i9-14900HX (8 performance and 16 efficiency cores, 32 threads), 62.5 GiB,
Fedora Linux 44 (kernel 6.19.10), Docker 29.8.2 with its data root on btrfs on a Samsung SSD 9100
PRO 8 TB. **Shared:** a KIND control plane ran beside every run, and another agent's ClickHouse
benchmark (`clickhouse server` and `kiln-chbench`, 4 to 7 CPUs) ran in cycles: during r2's
bootstrap (whether during its load was not sampled), through r3's load (at 10:07:59 it held ~7
CPUs and no core of 32 was idle), and during r4's bootstrap, ending between 10:21:28 and 10:21:48
(r4's `host-samples.txt`, every 20 s): in the first 13 s of r4's rest window (10:21:35 to
10:22:38), before its load (10:22:40 to 10:24:04). The host's load average before and after each
run is in its `host.txt`.

**Harness:** `tools/compare/compose/run.sh` in work mode, wrapped to hold the 33-node lock, run
under a compose project of its own and restore the host's neighbour limits after:

    NODES=33 NODE_MEMORY=512m MODE=work AGENTS=500 RAISE_HOST_LIMITS=1 \
      bash tools/compare/compose/run.sh focal 220 60 15 128 OUT

(r4 with `REST=60` besides.)

## What a run does

1. **33 nodes**, focal1 to focal33 over zones a, b and c (11 each), each limited to 0.90 of a CPU
   ((32 − 2) / 33, the generator holding 2) and 512 MiB. focal1 founds the cluster; the others join
   by invitation. The bootstrap raises the host's neighbour table for them (gc_thresh2 and 3 from
   512 and 1,024 to 1,222); the wrapper restores it after.
2. **11 sessions**, one for every node of the largest zone: the founder's and ten created on
   unseated zone-a nodes under the founder's tenant, each planned to survive a zone (three voters
   in three zones) and activated natively on the node that created it, its preferred leader.
   Every node holds a replica of exactly one (`<report>.sessions.json`: 11 of 11 placed, none
   bare).
3. **500 agents**, each an enrolled client and identity of its own, invited on the founder.
4. **Every session a ledger** (focal-load's `ledgers`, from `cluster.py ledgers`): ledger k is
   driven by callers k, k + 11, … (5 a ledger) among agents k, k + 11, … (45 or 46 a ledger),
   each agent homed on one of its session's three voters (reads and inbox waits there, writes at
   the leader). Each ledger is offered 1/11 of one schedule: 20 claims a second.
5. **The work flow**, 220 claims a second for 75 s (60 s measured after 15 s of warm-up): 16,500
   lifecycles, each a claim's eleven writes among three of its ledger's agents (issuer, worker,
   evaluator), each step learned of from the next agent's inbox (`tools/load/README.md`).
6. **The verdict**, focal-load's report: each lifecycle started ends written, refused, unknown,
   abandoned or undelivered; `judged` counts the claims settled satisfied.

## Found on the way, fixed at the cause

All in the harness; the cluster refused nothing, lost nothing and left nothing unknown.

- **The agents drove one session, homed over every node.** The work mode homed every agent on a
  node of `list nodes` and drove the founder's session alone, so at 33 nodes 30 of an agent's 33
  possible homes held no replica of the session it wrote, and ten sessions took no load. Each
  session is now a ledger, its agents homed on its own voters (`cluster.py ledgers`).
- **176 callers, past focal-load's 64.** The first run (r1-0925) gave each ledger the 16 callers a
  run of one has, and focal-load refused the shape before any write (`concurrency must be
  1..=64`). A ledger is offered its share of the rate whatever its callers, so each now takes as
  many as the 64 hold for each where that is fewer than 16 (5 here); a run with fewer than three
  agents a session, or more sessions than callers, is refused before it starts.
- **A node's replicas were read for the founder's session alone.** `inspect replicas` without
  `--session` reads a node's original session, which 30 of 33 nodes hold no replica of: step 4's
  records held 30 empty files a run. Each node's replicas are read for every session it holds.
- **The drive's counters were read as the nodes'.** On a shared host they hold every tenant's
  writes; each container's own (`<report>.io.txt`) and a rest window (`REST`) are recorded now.

## Open

- **69 KB written a committed write**, its three replicas together, ~23 KB a replica for an
  eleven-step lifecycle's write of a 128-byte payload: what each replica writes, and why, is not
  yet broken down (the log's records, the sealed payload log, checkpoints, the content store).
- **The founder at rest**, 0.236 of a CPU with 500 agents enrolled and 11 sessions placed, not
  yet broken down: it leads the root's 33 members, the directory partition (whose conditional
  reads, step 4's, still take a linearizable read round each; a read the leader holds until
  something changes is the step after) and its own session.
- **Enrolling 500 agents took about 6 minutes** (r1-0925: the sessions active by 09:30:23, the
  load begun by ~09:36), one invitation and one enrollment at a time, each invitation a root
  command all 33 root members apply.
- **focal-load's 64 callers** (`MAX_CONCURRENCY`) are said to be OS threads with their own client
  and runtime; since e276fc49 they are tasks on the run's one shard, so the bound's stated reason
  no longer holds. The measured runs took 55 of them.
