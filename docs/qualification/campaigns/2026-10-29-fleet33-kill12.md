# A dozen of 33 nodes killed and started again under the work flow (goal condition 3 at scale)

Date: 2026-10-29. focal at `fleet-run` 476ee125: the 33-node work flow ([its record](2026-10-29-fleet33-work.md),
24770d5c) and the seven fixes this campaign found, 33667142 to 476ee125. The binaries were built once on
the host as `focal:fleet-k7` from 476ee125 (`tree.txt`: the committed tree, nothing uncommitted).

**Host:** fedora (kiln runner kiln-a094a12d.attlocal.net, hostname fedora-2): Intel Core i9-14900HX
(32 threads), 62.5 GiB, Fedora Linux 44 (kernel 6.19.10), Docker 29.8.2 on btrfs on a Samsung SSD 9100
PRO. **Shared:** three kiln CI jobs ran on the host through the final run (its load average 18.0 before
and 27.3 after, `host.txt`), so its latencies are a busy host's.

**Harness:** `tools/compare/compose/run.sh` in work mode, as the 33-node record runs it, with a fault:

    NODES=33 NODE_MEMORY=512m MODE=work AGENTS=500 RAISE_HOST_LIMITS=1 \
      FAULTS='kill=12@45+30' FAULT_SEED=1029 \
      bash tools/compare/compose/run.sh focal 220 120 15 128 OUT

## What a run does

1. **The cluster and the load** as in the 33-node record: 33 nodes of 0.90 CPU and 512 MiB over three
   zones, 11 sessions on three voters in three zones each, 500 agents, every session a ledger,
   220 claims a second for 135 s (120 s measured after 15 s): 29,700 lifecycles of eleven writes.
2. **The kill.** 45 s into the load, twelve of the 33 nodes, drawn by the seed (the same twelve in
   every run), are killed at once (`docker kill`, SIGKILL): focal1 (the founder: the root's leader,
   the directory partition's and its own session's), focal6, 7, 8, 9, 16, 17, 23, 27, 28, 31 and 32.
   Each session loses its leader, a follower, its quorum (two or three of its voters) or nothing.
3. **The restart.** 30 s later they are started again (`docker start`), each probed until it serves
   (`inspect node --probe serving`). Every line of `<report>.faults.txt` names the Unix time in ms
   the report's CSV of writes is timed against.
4. **The verdict:** focal-load's report (each lifecycle written and judged, or refused by a cause,
   unknown, abandoned or undelivered; `resent`, the writes asked again under their own identity), the
   CSV (each write's start, latency and outcome), and each node's replicas, health and metrics after
   the load. `killreport.md` is all of it by ledger (`~/s5/killreport.py`, from the run's files).

## Result (k7-1551, the final tree)

All twelve were dead within 1.2 s of the kill, started again at 34.0 s, and served 0.3 to 2.8 s
after their start (all by 36.8 s).

**Every lifecycle completed or was refused while nodes were down.** The 105 refusals were writes begun
0.2 to 24.0 s after the kill: 84 answered before the restart, the other 21 (all in ledger 8, which
lost two of its three voters) after it and before every killed node served again. `unavailable`
(92): `claim.submit` in ledger 8 while it had no quorum. `owner_bound` (13): `validation.begin` refused
by its custody check, naming the copy missing ("custody: N of M required copies hold the artifact;
missing nodes …"), in sessions whose content copy was killed. Nothing else was refused.

**None lost or unknown.** 421 writes were ones whose client could not learn their outcome within its
30 s clock (their session had lost its leader or its quorum as they were sent): each agent sent its
write again under its own identity until the ledger answered it, and every one was answered,
committed once (`resent`). No write ended unknown, no lifecycle's read failed for good (abandoned) and
no hand-off went unseen (undelivered). Every judged lifecycle's eleven writes were each read by the
next step. After the load, every session's three voters had applied and committed the same index and
published the same count, the restarted ones included.

## Found, fixed at the cause

1. **A restarted node's failure detector held no member** (aec252ec). Its members came from the
   partitions the placement agent observed at the end of a pass, and on a restarted node an earlier
   step failed every pass (reopening its copies, a root intent, a partition read), so it probed no one,
   never refuted its death, and the survivors, which revive a member only at a higher incarnation, held
   it dead to the end: every artifact job needing a copy on it was refused. Members are now read from
   the root the node holds, at the start of every pass.
2. **The agent asked a fixed node of a partition led elsewhere** (aec252ec): the founder, else the first
   live voter, whatever the group answered; once the founder's death moved the directory partition's
   leadership, every agent whose pick was a follower asked it ~100 times, observing nothing. The agent
   now follows the leader a `NotLeader` answer names and moves past a node that gives no answer.
3. **A restarted directory partition voter was refused its permit for good** (4c9fbede): the permit
   refused a root authority snapshot past one control command (128 KiB), though only a founder alone in
   its group proposes it; the root's authority grows ~175 B with every enrolled client, so with 500 agents
   the partition's two restarted voters were refused ~690 times each and the partition never had a leader
   again.
4. **A session stopped when its new leader handed leadership back** (33667142): a participant's write
   the log dropped during a leadership transfer (`LeaderLeaving`) was classed fail-closed, and the
   replica of a survivor elected leader stopped ("a leader does not remove itself") when the restarted
   preferred leader took over; its session kept no quorum.
5. **A client kept a dead route for its whole clock** (fb570631): a write in flight to a killed leader
   was resent to it until its 30 s clock ran out, then reported unknown, though its session had another
   leader within a second. A route that gives no answer is now held failed and the request resent
   through another: the node that names the leader, or the next of the client's given routes.
6. **Agents gave up at the client's clock** (5b2aa3ca): a write whose outcome was unknown, or a read no
   node answered, ended its lifecycle (unknown, abandoned) though its session would answer it once its
   voters returned. An agent now reaches its ledger through every node holding it, resends such a write
   under its own identity (its generation held open, so the ledger answers it once) and asks such a
   read again, up to a hand-off's wait (120 s).
7. **The run said too little of a fault** (476ee125): fault times in the report's clock, each restarted
   node's time to serve, and every node's health and log end.

## Open

- **Ledger 9 lost its leader and kept a quorum, yet wrote nothing for 35.2 s.** focal15, a survivor
  that is not a content copy, led term 2 (it handed leadership back to focal28 at term 3, and refused
  1,732 artifact jobs at once as the dead copy's), but none of the ledger's writes committed from 3 s
  after the kill until the restart; ledger 10, killed alike (its leader and a copy), wrote again in
  10.5 s. Not yet root-caused: the next fault campaign samples each such session's survivors.
- **The all-copies custody rule** (doc 26 §1): an artifact job waits for, or is refused without, every
  content copy, so a session whose copy is down refuses those steps until it returns (13 refusals here);
  acknowledging at f+1 copies is the user's decision, pending.
- **Failover detection is the 10 s QUIC idle timeout**: a session that lost its leader took 10.0 to
  10.5 s to write again.
- **The directory partition's authority refresh** carries the root's whole enrollment registry in one
  128 KiB command: about 500 enrolled clients is its ceiling.
