# A dozen of 33 nodes killed and started again under the work flow (goal condition 3 at scale)

Date: 2026-10-29. focal at `fleet-run` 5a7af464: the 33-node work flow ([its record](2026-10-29-fleet33-work.md),
24770d5c) and the eleven fixes this campaign found, 33667142 to 5a7af464. The binaries of the two final
runs were built once on the host as `focal:fleet-k14` from 5a7af464 (`tree.txt`: the committed tree).

**Host:** fedora (kiln runner kiln-a094a12d.attlocal.net, hostname fedora-2): Intel Core i9-14900HX
(32 threads), 62.5 GiB, Fedora Linux 44 (kernel 6.19.10), Docker 29.8.2 on btrfs on a Samsung SSD 9100
PRO. **Shared:** kiln CI jobs ran on the host all evening, and another tenant's ClickHouse benchmark held
it at a load of 500 to 600 until minutes before k14 began (its five-minute load average 240 at k14's
start, 34 at k15's), so the latencies are a busy host's.

**Harness:** `tools/compare/compose/run.sh` in work mode, as the 33-node record runs it, with a fault:

    NODES=33 NODE_MEMORY=512m MODE=work AGENTS=500 RAISE_HOST_LIMITS=1 \
      FAULTS='kill=12@45+30' FAULT_SEED=1029 \
      bash tools/compare/compose/run.sh focal 220 120 15 128 OUT

## What a run does

1. **The cluster and the load** as in the 33-node record: 33 nodes of 0.90 CPU and 512 MiB over three
   zones, 11 sessions on three voters in three zones each (placed afresh each run), 500 agents, every
   session a ledger, 220 claims a second for 135 s (120 s measured after 15 s): 29,700 lifecycles.
2. **The kill.** 45 s into the load, twelve of the 33 nodes, drawn by the seed (the same twelve every
   run), are killed at once (`docker kill`, SIGKILL): focal1 (the founder: the root's leader, the
   directory partition's and its own session's), focal6, 7, 8, 9, 16, 17, 23, 27, 28, 31 and 32. Each
   session loses its leader, a follower, its quorum (two or three voters) or nothing.
3. **The restart.** 30 s later they are started again (`docker start`), each probed until it serves.
   Every line of `<report>.faults.txt` names the Unix time in ms the report's CSV is timed against.
4. **The verdict:** focal-load's report (each lifecycle written and judged, or refused by a cause,
   unknown, abandoned or undelivered; `resent`: writes asked again under their own identity), the CSV
   (each write's start, latency and outcome), and each node's replicas, health, metrics and log end
   after the load. `killreport.md` is all of it by ledger, each ledger's longest time without a commit
   with where it fell (from the run's files).

in k15. In k14 each was answered while they were down too (110 before the restart, 11 before the last of
them served). In k15, 290 and 41 were, and 208 were answered within 1.4 s after the last one served:
artifact writes begun 10.5 to 11.9 s after the kill whose 30 s clocks ran out as their copy came back.

**None lost or unknown.** The resent writes were ones whose client could not learn their outcome within
its 30 s clock (their session had lost its leader or quorum as they were sent); each agent sent its
write again under its own identity until the ledger answered, and each was answered once. No write
ended unknown, no lifecycle's read failed for good, no hand-off went unseen, and every written
lifecycle's eleven writes committed and its claim was seen satisfied. After the load every voter of
every session had applied and committed the same index with no seed chunk or content object missing
(both runs, all 11 sessions); no replica had stopped, no node held a member dead and no placement agent
was failing.

A session that lost its leader elected another within 1.8 to 2.1 s (`sessions-sampled.txt`); its
clients reached the new leader 10 s after the kill, when their connections to the dead one reached
focal-wire's QUIC idle timeout. One that lost its quorum wrote again once its voters served. An
artifact step needs every content copy of its session (the all-copies custody rule, 26 §1): its node
refuses it while it holds a copy dead and the client asks again for up to its 30 s clock, so lifecycles
at that step wait for the copy while the session's other writes commit. focal-load holds 320 lifecycles
a ledger (5 callers of 64), which fill at that step 15 to 21 s after the kill; the ledger then takes no
write until the first of them is answered, refused as its clock runs out (from 30.3 s) or committed
once the copy is back. Every ledger's writes begun from 51 to 89 s after the kill on (k14), 83 to 98 s
(k15), were none slower than its slowest before it.

## Found, fixed at the cause

1. **A restarted node's failure detector held no member** (aec252ec). Its members came from the
   partitions the placement agent observed at the end of a pass, and on a restarted node an earlier
   step failed every pass, so it probed no one, never refuted its death, and the survivors (which revive
   a member only at a higher incarnation) held it dead to the end, refusing every artifact job needing a
   copy on it. Members are read from the root the node holds, at the start of every pass.
2. **The agent asked a fixed node of a partition led elsewhere** (aec252ec): the founder, else the first
   live voter, whatever the group answered; once the founder's death moved the directory partition's
   leadership, every agent whose pick was a follower asked it ~100 times, observing nothing. The agent
   follows the leader a `NotLeader` answer names and moves past a node that gives no answer.
3. **A restarted directory partition voter was refused its permit for good** (4c9fbede): the permit
   refused a root authority snapshot past one control command (128 KiB), though only a founder alone in
   its group proposes it; the root's authority grows ~175 B with every enrolled client, so with 500
   agents the partition's two restarted voters were refused ~690 times each and it never led again.
4. **A session stopped when its new leader handed leadership back** (33667142): a participant's write
   the log dropped during a leadership transfer (`LeaderLeaving`) was classed fail-closed, so the
   replica of a survivor elected leader stopped when the restarted preferred leader took over.
5. **A client kept a dead route for its whole clock** (fb570631): a write in flight to a killed leader
   was resent to it until its 30 s clock ran out, then reported unknown, though its session had another
   leader within a second. A route that gives no answer is held failed and the request resent through
   another: the node that names the leader, or the next of the client's given routes.
6. **Agents gave up at the client's clock** (5b2aa3ca): a write whose outcome was unknown, or a read no
   node answered, ended its lifecycle though its session answered once its voters returned. An agent
   reaches its ledger through every node holding it, resends such a write under its own identity (its
   generation held open, so the ledger answers it once) and asks such a read again, up to a hand-off's
   wait (120 s).
7. **The run said too little of a fault** (476ee125): fault times in the report's clock, each restarted
   node's time to serve, and every node's health and log end.
8. **A node's route cache read every partition at the founder** (1ec045e1), so with the founder dead,
   or no longer leading, a follower named no new leader to its clients and a node without its copy yet
   answered them `Unavailable`: in k8 a session whose new leader was ready in 2 s took no write for 38.5
   s, and agents homed where no route could be resolved were refused 1,733 writes and abandoned 332
   reads. The cache reads each partition at the leader it has learned and moves past one that gives no
   answer, as the agent does (2).
9. **A client sent every request to its home whatever the last one there found** (1d9d8237): each read
   and each wait for its events of an agent homed on a killed node waited out the connection's idle
   timeout (10 s) there before the next voter answered it, request after request until its home was
   back, so in k9 and k10 every session that lost a voter but kept its quorum went 7.3 to 8.2 s without
   a commit 22 to 30 s after the kill. A given route that gave no answer is passed over for the next,
   as a Kafka consumer whose fetch from its preferred replica failed fetches from the leader (KIP-392),
   and reached again beside a request sent elsewhere, never by one; the endpoint's owner no longer
   counts a caller that stopped waiting among a dial's waiters.
10. **No replica could restore a checkpoint past about 2,100 artifacts** (26e7a9a2): each artifact is
    prepaid its verifier's declared maximum of restore work (76.6 M units under the built-in schemas)
    and a restore's allowance grows by that per artifact, but never past the configuration's ceiling,
    which carried no such term. In k9, k11, k12 and k13 a restarted voter, or a member new to a
    session, stopped ("native lifecycle admission: a configured bound or counter would be exceeded"),
    and the diagnostic runs placed the stop in a snapshot's restore: in k13 every snapshot near 1,480
    artifacts in 61,000 rows restored with 6.4e9 of its 1.23e11 left, and the one taken at index
    12,368 ran out while its rows were rebuilt. The ceiling holds the custody of the most artifact rows
    its checkpoint may hold.
11. **A replica behind its reader answered from its prefix** (5a7af464): a list of events after a
    record it had not applied said nothing came after, and an inbox wait held until something did. In
    k12 a restarted follower whose snapshot install waited on a seed chunk stayed at index 12,374 while
    its leader reached 29,902, and the agents homed on it saw none of their hand-offs: thirteen
    lifecycles ended undelivered and their ledger took no write for 60 s. It is refused
    `Behind { published }`, and a homed read asks the leader as for any read its home is behind on.

## Open

- **A restarted follower's snapshot install waited on a seed chunk to the end** (k12-1906, focal9,
  session d4bdaaf2: applied 12,374 of its leader's 29,902, one seed chunk missing; `diag.txt`). No peer
  sweeps a seed within the collector's 24 h grace, so each pull of it was refused or its install failed,
  and the node keeps neither reason. Since fix 11 its homed agents ask the leader and lose nothing; the
  replica stays behind. Once in eleven runs.
- **The all-copies custody rule** (26 §1): an artifact job waits for, or is refused without, every
  content copy, so a session whose copy is down holds those steps until it returns; acknowledging at
  f+1 copies is the user's decision, pending.
- **Failover detection is the 10 s QUIC idle timeout**: a session that lost its leader wrote again 10 s
  after the kill though its new leader was ready within 2.1 s.
- **The evidence door under resends of jobs refused for a dead copy**: in k11 it held artifact jobs
  until the session's request timeout and refused 36 `ingress_capacity` (none in k12 to k15).
- **The directory partition's authority refresh** carries the root's whole enrollment registry in one
  128 KiB command: about 500 enrolled clients is its ceiling.
